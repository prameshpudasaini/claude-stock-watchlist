## The Big Picture Today (read this first)

The market is trying to catch its breath after an ugly October, and there's a lot packed into this single week. Here's the plain-English rundown:

- **October was rough.** The Nasdaq-100 (an index of the 100 biggest non-bank companies, heavy on tech) fell about **6.5%** last month — its worst month since early 2025. The big reason: giant technology companies are spending staggering amounts of money building artificial-intelligence (AI) systems, and investors are nervous that all that spending is eating into profits. Google's parent, **Alphabet**, dropped nearly **10%** last week after its results disappointed. Microsoft's results, by contrast, were good.

- **The Federal Reserve just made things more tense.** The Fed (the U.S. central bank that sets interest rates) **held rates steady** last week at a range of 3.75%–4.00%, and the new chair, Kevin Warsh, refused to say what comes next. Here's the surprising part: instead of expecting a rate *cut*, traders now think there's roughly a **70%–80% chance of a rate INCREASE in December**. Higher interest rates are generally bad for risky, fast-growing stocks, so this is a worry hanging over everything.

- **Elections are tomorrow.** The U.S. midterm elections are **Tuesday, November 3**. Big political events can make the market jumpy for a day or two, so don't be shocked by extra swings this week.

- **It's a huge earnings week.** A wave of major companies report their quarterly results over the next few days, including **Palantir (tonight, after the market closes)**, **AMD**, **Qualcomm**, **Pfizer**, and **McDonald's**. Each one can move not just its own stock but the whole mood.

- **This morning looks slightly green.** Early bets (futures) were modestly higher before the open — S&P 500 up about **0.4%**, the Nasdaq-100 up about **0.9%**, the Dow up about **0.3%** — a small bounce-back attempt after the rough stretch.

- **Bitcoin is still weak.** Bitcoin is trading in roughly the **high-$60,000s**, down nearly **half** from its October record near $126,000. That keeps pressure on crypto-related stocks like Coinbase and Strategy (formerly MicroStrategy).

**What this means for you:** The mood is fragile and this week is event-heavy (elections, a pile of earnings, Fed worries). That's a recipe for sharp moves in both directions. Keep trades small, and be extra careful about buying anything the day before it reports earnings — you're essentially gambling on a coin flip.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

### 1. Inovio (INO) — a massive pop already happened; watch, don't chase
- **Move:** Up more than **200%** before the open (to around $2 a share, from well under $1).
- **Why:** This is the big story of the morning. On Friday, the FDA approved Inovio's **first-ever product** — a treatment called Voceryq for a rare throat disease (recurrent respiratory papillomatosis, where growths keep coming back in the airway). A tiny biotech company getting its very first drug approved is genuinely transformative news, which is why the stock tripled.
- **Plain-English take:** The news is real and important, but the stock has **already shot up 200%+**. When something triples overnight, a big chunk of the good news is already in the price, and these pops very often give back a large piece in the days after. **For most people: just watch it today, don't chase it higher.** If you already somehow own it, taking some profit into a spike like this is reasonable. **Risk:** it's still a small company with one approved product and a required follow-up study to prove the drug works long-term; excitement can fade fast.

### 2. Palantir (PLTR) — reports tonight; wait for the number before betting
- **Move:** Roughly flat to slightly higher in early trading; the real action comes after today's close.
- **Why:** Palantir, one of the most popular AI software companies, reports its quarterly results **tonight after the market closes**. Traders are betting the stock could swing about **10% in either direction** on the news. In a nervous market that just punished Alphabet for a weak report, expectations here are sky-high.
- **Plain-English take:** Buying right before a report like this is a gamble, not a plan — if the numbers disappoint even slightly, a richly-priced stock like this can drop hard. **The smarter move is to wait, watch how it reacts, and consider buying tomorrow only if the results are strong and the stock holds its gains.** Let the dust settle first.

### 3. CrowdStrike (CRWD) — consider buying on strength
- **Move:** Up about **3%–3.5%** before the open.
- **Why:** This cybersecurity company got a vote of confidence from a Wall Street firm (an upgrade by Deutsche Bank). Cybersecurity has been one of the more resilient corners of tech because companies keep spending on security no matter what the economy does.
- **Plain-English take:** On a shaky day for tech, a name that's going *up* on its own good news is worth a look. **If it keeps climbing and the overall market stays calm, this is a reasonable one to consider buying** for a short bounce. **Risk:** it's still a pricey tech stock, so if the whole group rolls over again on AI-spending fears, it won't be immune.

### 4. Rivian (RIVN) — consider buying, modest and news-driven
- **Move:** Up about **4%** before the open.
- **Why:** The electric-truck maker was upgraded to a "buy" rating by a Wall Street firm (UBS). It's a specific, positive piece of news on a stock that's been beaten down.
- **Plain-English take:** This is a smaller, cleaner idea than the big tech names. **If it holds its gains through the morning, it's a candidate to consider buying** for a short move higher. **Risk:** Rivian is still a money-losing company in a tough EV market, and the stock is volatile — keep the position small.

### 5. Precigen (PGEN) — beaten down by a rival's news; watch for an oversold bounce
- **Move:** Down roughly **19%–27%**.
- **Why:** This is the flip side of the Inovio story. Precigen already sells the leading approved treatment (Papzimeos) for the same rare throat disease — and now Inovio's newly-approved drug becomes a direct competitor. Investors dumped the stock on the fear of lost sales.
- **Plain-English take:** The competitive worry is real, but Precigen's drug is already on the market and selling (about $75 million in the first half of this year), so a **27% wipeout may be an overreaction.** **This is a watch-for-a-bounce idea:** if the selling stops and it starts to steady over the next day or two, it could snap back some. **Risk:** don't try to catch it while it's still falling, and understand the new competition is a genuine long-term headwind.
