*Short-term trading ideas for holding 1 to 14 days. Written in plain English.*

This is the single biggest event of the week, and the whole market is holding its breath. ## Top 5 Picks for Today

### 1. Nvidia (NVDA) — roughly flat in early trading, reports results tonight after the close
This is the main event. Nvidia has been beaten down hard and expectations are all over the place. Tonight's report will likely move not just Nvidia but the entire market, and the stock itself could easily swing 8–12% in either direction once the numbers come out.

**What to do:** Be honest with yourself — buying or betting against a stock in the final hours before its big report is a gamble, not an investment. For most people the smart move is to **wait and watch**: let the results come out tonight and then decide tomorrow morning when you can see how the stock and the whole market react. If you already own it and believe AI spending stays huge, you can hold through the announcement, but understand you are accepting a big overnight swing. Do not put money in today that you would be upset to lose by tomorrow.

### 2. CG Oncology (CGON) — up about 33% to roughly $99 (from about $74)
This is the cleanest good-news story today. CG Oncology reported that its late-stage study of a bladder-cancer treatment worked well — it cut the chance of the cancer coming back by 68%. Strong trial results like this often keep lifting a biotech stock for several days as analysts raise their opinions.

**What to do:** Consider buying if it holds its big jump, because the trial result is genuinely strong and this kind of news tends to have staying power. The warning: the stock already leapt a lot, so you are buying after the pop, and biotech stocks can reverse quickly if doubts creep in or the next update disappoints. Keep your position modest and don't chase it if it has run up even further by the time you look.

### 3. Aytu BioPharma (AYTU) — up about 45% to roughly $2.64 on a buyout offer
Aytu, a small drug company, agreed to be bought by Rosemont Pharmaceuticals for $2.70 a share — a big jump over where it was trading last week. When a company agrees to be bought, its shares usually climb toward the offer price and then sit there until the deal closes.

**What to do:** This is a lower-drama idea. The stock is unlikely to go much above the $2.70 offer price, so the big upside is already mostly gone — **do not pay more than about $2.70**, because above that you'd be betting on a higher offer that may never come. The small risk is that the deal falls apart, which would send the stock back down. If you can buy clearly below $2.70, it's a relatively steady way to pocket the gap as the deal (expected to close in late December) moves ahead.

### 4. Home Depot (HD) — up about 2% after a solid earnings report
The big home-improvement retailer reported sales of $43.4 billion, which beat what Wall Street expected ($42.8 billion), and it nudged up its sales forecast for the year. This is a calm, steady, large company — the opposite of the risky small stocks elsewhere on this list.

**What to do:** Consider buying if it holds its gains after the report. This is the kind of safer, lower-excitement idea that can drift higher over the next week or two without wild swings. The warning: a big retailer won't make you rich quickly, and if the overall market falls on a bad Nvidia report tonight, even good stocks like this can get pulled down for a day or two.

### 5. Red Cat Holdings (RCAT) — down about 26% to 30% on a bad report (a stock to bet against or avoid)
Red Cat, which makes drones for the military, reported sales of only about $11.8 million — far below the roughly $42 million Wall Street expected — and it pulled its sales forecast for the year entirely. That is a serious disappointment, and the company losing confidence in its own forecast is a red flag.

**What to do:** This is one to **bet against if it keeps falling, or simply stay away from**. A miss this large plus a withdrawn forecast often means the stock keeps sliding for several days as disappointed investors sell. Betting against a stock (hoping it falls further) is risky because beaten-down stocks can bounce sharply for no clear reason, so if you try it, keep it small. For most people, the easier choice is just to avoid it.
