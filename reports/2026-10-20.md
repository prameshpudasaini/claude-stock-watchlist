## What's going on in the market today (the big picture)

- **It's a huge earnings morning, and the results are mostly good — but one big name got punished.** General Motors, 3M, RTX (the defense and jet-engine company), GE Aerospace, Halliburton, and Coca-Cola all reported their quarterly results before the opening bell. Investors are now nervous that some borrowed too much. This morning that fear got a fresh poke from overseas: a South Korean AI data-center company called **Elice** had its first day of trading and immediately crashed about 30%, even though small investors had clamored to buy it. When a hot new AI company flops on day one, it tells you the mood toward the whole AI-building boom has turned cautious.
- **CoreWeave keeps falling.** CoreWeave (which rents out AI computing power and has borrowed heavily to do it) dropped to a new 52-week low around **$58.58** this morning. A big bank, HSBC, repeated its "sell" view with a price target of just $39, warning that CoreWeave's borrowing costs are creeping toward a painful 15%. This stock is the market's thermometer for AI-debt fear.
- **Futures were mixed, with chips weak.** Before the bell, Dow futures were slightly up, but the tech-heavy Nasdaq futures were down about half a percent as computer-chip stocks slipped again. (Futures are bets on where the market opens.)
- **Oil is jumping.** Brent crude (a main oil price) pushed above **$111 a barrel**, partly on worries about conflict in the Middle East, including a threat from Iran to target undersea cables. Higher oil helps energy companies but can spook the broader market.
- **Two big reports land after today's close.** Netflix reports its results this afternoon (around 4 p.m. New York time), and Tesla reports tomorrow (Wednesday) afternoon. Both can swing hard overnight.

**The one-sentence takeaway:** Solid company earnings are fighting against a nervous "is the AI boom overbuilt?" mood — so lean toward the boring winners with real results, keep AI-debt bets small, and remember that oil and defense are catching a bid.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

### 1. RTX (ticker: RTX) — consider buying
- **Move:** Up in pre-market trading after a strong report.
- **Why:** This defense and jet-engine maker (formerly Raytheon) blew past expectations, earning $2.26 per share versus the roughly $1.78 that analysts expected — a very large beat. It also raised its forecast for the full year and pointed to a big backlog of orders. On top of that, fresh Middle East tensions and talk of attacks on undersea cables tend to push money toward defense companies. Two tailwinds at once.
- **What to do:** A sensible **buy** for a short run on a genuine earnings win plus the defense mood. **Risk:** the stock may have already jumped on the news before you can get in, so don't chase it if it has run up a lot; defense stocks can drift back down once the headlines cool.

### 2. 3M (ticker: MMM) — consider buying
- **Move:** Up roughly **3% to 5%** in pre-market trading, around $168.
- **Why:** The maker of Post-it Notes, tape, and industrial products beat on profit ($2.50 per share vs. about $2.39 expected) and raised its full-year profit outlook for the second quarter in a row. It also announced an expanded deal with Amazon tied to data centers, even handing Amazon a warrant (a right to buy 3M stock) worth more than $500 million — a vote of confidence that gave the stock extra lift.
- **What to do:** A reasonable **buy** for a few days on a clean beat-and-raise. **Risk:** sales came in a touch light versus expectations, and 3M is a slow-and-steady industrial company, so the upside here is modest, not explosive.

### 3. General Motors (ticker: GM) — be careful; a bet-against could work short-term
- **Move:** Down about **6% to 7%** in pre-market trading, falling from around $84 to near $77.
- **Why:** This is the odd one out. GM actually beat expectations ($3.70 per share vs. $3.63) and announced a giant $20 billion plan to buy back its own shares. But investors wanted a third straight raise to its full-year outlook and didn't get one — GM only narrowed its forecast, and the middle of that range came in slightly below what Wall Street hoped. When a "good" report still disappoints, the stock often keeps sliding for a day or two.
- **What to do:** This could be one to **bet against** (you profit if it keeps falling) for a very short trade while the disappointment sinks in. **Big risk:** that enormous $20 billion buyback is real support — the company itself will be buying shares, which can put a floor under the price and snap it back up. This is a quick trade, not a conviction call; cap your losses.

### 4. CoreWeave (ticker: CRWV) — consider betting against, but very high risk
- **Move:** Down to a new 52-week low around **$58.58** in pre-market trading.
- **Why:** CoreWeave is the face of the "AI debt" scare. It rents out AI computing power and has borrowed billions to build it. HSBC just repeated its "sell" rating with a $39 target, warning that slowing AI demand and rising borrowing costs (near 15%) could squeeze the company. The flop of the Elice data-center IPO overseas this morning only deepened those worries.
- **What to do:** One to **bet against** for a short trade while the AI-debt fear runs hot. **Big risk:** this stock is a rollercoaster. Just yesterday Goldman Sachs upgraded it to "buy," and chip giant Nvidia (which owns a piece of it) has publicly said CoreWeave's finances are improving. Any reassuring headline can rocket it up 10%+ in a day. Keep it tiny and set a firm limit on your losses.

### 5. Halliburton (ticker: HAL) — consider buying
- **Move:** Up about **3%** in pre-market trading.
- **Why:** This oilfield-services company (it helps drillers get oil and gas out of the ground) beat profit expectations this morning. And the timing is good: oil prices are surging, with Brent crude pushing above $111. Higher oil prices usually mean oil companies spend more on drilling, which flows straight to Halliburton's business.
- **What to do:** A reasonable **buy** for a short run that rides both a real earnings beat and rising oil. **Risk:** oil prices are jumpy and news-driven — if the Middle East tension eases and oil drops back, energy stocks can cool off just as fast.
