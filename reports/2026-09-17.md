## The big picture this morning (read this first)

Yesterday was a big day, and today is the morning after. Here is what happened and what it means for you:

1. **The Federal Reserve raised interest rates yesterday (Wednesday, Sept. 16) — its first rate increase in three years.** The Fed lifted its main rate by a quarter of a percentage point, to a range of 3.75%–4%, to fight inflation that has been stoked by expensive oil. Even more important, the Fed hinted that *another* increase could come before the end of the year. Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh sounded firm and serious about beating inflation. Higher rates make borrowing more expensive and are generally bad news for pricey technology and fast-growing companies. The Dow dropped 631 points (about 1.2%) on the news, the S&P 500 fell about 0.5%, and the tech-heavy Nasdaq finished roughly flat.

2. **This morning the market is trying to bounce back.** Before the opening bell today, stock futures (early bets on where the market will open) are pointing higher: the Dow up about 0.2%, the S&P 500 up about 0.3%, and the Nasdaq up about 0.5%. In plain terms, after a scary day, some investors decided the Fed's tough talk on inflation was actually reassuring, and they are nibbling back in. Do not assume the bounce lasts — with the Fed warning of more hikes, days like this can turn choppy fast.

3. **The interest rate on the 10-year U.S. government bond is back above 5%, near its highest in years.** When you can earn 5% safely from a government bond, expensive tech stocks look less attractive by comparison. This is a steady weight on the most expensive corners of the market.

4. **Oil is still expensive and Bitcoin is still weak.** High oil prices (tied to Middle East tensions) are part of why the Fed is worried about inflation — and they help oil-company stocks while hurting most others. Bitcoin is stuck around $76,000, below the $77,000–$78,000 area it needs to get back above to look healthy again. Crypto-linked stocks stay under pressure.

Bottom line: the market is bruised but trying to recover this morning. Money keeps favoring things that do well when rates and oil are high (energy) and specific companies with their own good news, while punishing rate-sensitive areas like homebuilders. Expect a jumpy day.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

**1. Intel (ticker: INTC) — riding a genuine turnaround story. Consider buying, but you're chasing a stock that has already run.**
Intel makes computer chips, and after years of struggling it is suddenly the comeback story of the chip world. An analyst firm (Northland) recently upgraded the stock with a $120 target, and the shares have jumped hard — including about 10% on news about "Terafab," a giant chip-factory project Intel is working on with Tesla, Elon Musk's SpaceX, and his AI company xAI. On top of that, there is a shortage of the server chips Intel makes, and the company has been raising prices. All of that is real, near-term good news that can keep the stock moving. **The catch:** it has already climbed a lot in a short time, so you would be buying after a big run — and if the whole market rolls back over on more rate worries, even winners get pulled down. Keep the position small.

**2. Coherent (ticker: COHR) — a fresh piece of good news is lifting it. Consider buying on the momentum.**
Coherent makes the optical parts (think super-fast light-based connections) that AI data centers need, so it rides the AI-spending wave. The stock jumped after the company named a well-regarded new boss — Jim Anderson, the former CEO of chip company Lattice Semiconductor — as its new chief executive. Investors like it when a proven leader takes over, and that excitement can carry a stock for several days. **The risk:** the pop is built on hope about a new leader, not on results you can see yet, so if the broader AI-chip mood sours, the enthusiasm can fade quickly.

**3. Lennar (ticker: LEN) — a rough earnings report just landed. This is one to bet against or simply avoid.**
Lennar is the second-biggest homebuilder in America, and yesterday afternoon it reported weaker results than Wall Street expected — less revenue *and* less profit than hoped — because the housing market is softening. Worse timing is hard to imagine: it reported just hours after the Fed raised rates, and higher rates mean higher mortgage costs, which makes it harder for people to buy homes. That is a direct headwind for a homebuilder. If you think the stock keeps sliding as investors digest the bad news, this could be one to bet against; the simpler move for most beginners is just to stay away from homebuilders while rates are climbing. **The catch:** a lot of bad news may already be in the price, so a beaten-down stock can bounce on any small piece of good news — don't oversize this.

**4. Energy Select Sector SPDR Fund (ticker: XLE) — the easy way to profit from expensive oil. Consider buying while oil stays high.**
The Fed itself pointed to high oil prices as a reason inflation is stubborn. Expensive oil is bad for most companies but *good* for oil producers, who make more money when oil sells for more. XLE is a basket of big oil and gas stocks, so buying it spreads your bet across the whole group instead of picking one company. If you'd rather own a single strong driller, **Diamondback Energy (ticker: FANG)** is a solid one. **The risk:** if the Middle East situation calms down, oil can fall back fast, and these stocks would drop with it. This is a "ride it while the news is hot" idea, not a forever hold.

**5. Micron Technology (ticker: MU) — memory-chip prices are rising. Consider buying as a bet on that shortage.**
Micron makes memory chips (the parts that store data), and there is a real shortage building: Wall Street expects memory-chip prices to jump more than 20% and the squeeze to last into 2027. Higher prices for what Micron sells usually means bigger profits. The company reports its latest results on September 30, so there is a clear near-term event that could push the stock. **The catch:** buying ahead of an earnings report is a gamble on the outcome — if the report or the company's forecast disappoints, the stock can drop sharply in a single day. If you're nervous about that, it's fine to watch and wait until after the report.
