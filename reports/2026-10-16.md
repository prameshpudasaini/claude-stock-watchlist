## What's going on in the market today (the big picture)

- **The biggest story is fear about "AI debt."** For two years, companies tied to artificial intelligence have been borrowing enormous amounts of money to build computer-chip data centers. This week, investors suddenly got nervous that some of them borrowed too much. The poster child is **CoreWeave**, a company that rents out AI computing power — the cost to insure its debt against default has shot up to levels that normally mean "this company might not pay its bills." That scared the whole AI corner of the market.
- **Good news this morning:** Those AI worries calmed down a little, so stock futures pointed slightly higher before the opening bell. But both the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq are still on track to finish the week lower.
- **Inflation is running hot.** Reports this week showed prices rising faster than expected (both the everyday-shopper number and the wholesale number that comes before it). That normally means the Federal Reserve (the group that sets interest rates) might raise rates again.
- **But the Fed is not panicking.** Fed Chair Kevin Warsh did not signal any rush to raise rates, so investors dialed back their bets on a hike. Most now expect the Fed to wait until its December meeting before deciding on a possible second increase this year.
- **It's bank-earnings week, and results are mixed.** Several regional banks reported this morning — some beat on profit but slipped on weaker sales.
- **Small companies are weak.** The Russell 2000 (an index of smaller companies) just fell below an important long-term trend line for the first time since March — a sign that the little guys are struggling even while big names hold up.

**The one-sentence takeaway:** This is a jumpy, nervous market where anything tied to heavy borrowing or crypto can swing hard — so keep positions small, be picky, and don't chase.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

### 1. CoreWeave (ticker: CRWV) — consider betting against, but very high risk
- **Move:** Down roughly **5%** early Friday, after a wild week. It crashed about 15% on Wednesday, bounced about 6% Thursday to around $70, and is sliding again.
- **Why:** CoreWeave rents out AI computing power and has borrowed more than $50 billion to do it. The cost to insure its bonds against default has spiked to "distress" levels — the bond market is now pricing in roughly a 63% chance it fails to pay over five years, up from about 30% a year ago. As long as investors are scared about AI borrowing, this stock is the lightning rod.
- **What to do:** This is one to **bet against** (you profit if it keeps falling) for a short trade while the fear lasts. **Big risk:** this stock is a rollercoaster. Chip giant Nvidia owns about 10% of it, the company insists its $100 billion order backlog covers all its debts, and at least one big bank (Deutsche Bank) is calling the drop a buying chance. Any reassuring headline could send it rocketing back up 10%+ in a single day. Keep it tiny and set a firm limit on your losses.

### 2. Tesla (ticker: TSLA) — consider buying
- **Move:** Up about **3% to 5%** in pre-market trading this morning.
- **Why:** Tesla unveiled its second-generation Roadster (a flashy new sports car), and investors liked the excitement. In a nervous market, a feel-good product reveal gave the stock a lift while most tech names struggled.
- **What to do:** A reasonable **buy** for a short ride on the good-news momentum. **Risk:** the actual car isn't expected to start production until 2028, so this is pure hype for now, not real sales. If the broader AI and tech jitters flare back up, Tesla can give back this pop quickly — it's a volatile stock.

### 3. Alcoa (ticker: AA) — consider buying
- **Move:** Up about **2.5%** after its quarterly report.
- **Why:** The aluminum maker earned $1.82 per share, crushing the roughly $1.54 that analysts expected. Metals prices have been firm, and the company delivered a clean, strong beat — exactly the kind of boring-but-solid result that holds up when the rest of the market is anxious.
- **What to do:** A sensible **buy** for a few days on a genuine earnings win. **Risk:** metals companies live and die by commodity prices and the health of the global economy, so a sudden growth scare or a stronger dollar could cool it off. The upside here is steady, not explosive.

### 4. Strategy (ticker: MSTR) — consider betting against
- **Move:** Down about **3%** in pre-market trading.
- **Why:** This company (formerly MicroStrategy) is basically a giant pile of Bitcoin wrapped in a stock, so it rises and falls with crypto. Two things are weighing on it: Bitcoin dipped below $80,000 earlier this week before steadying around $82,000, and there's a worry that a major index provider (MSCI) could remove the stock from its indexes — which would force some big funds to sell it.
- **What to do:** One to **bet against** for a short trade while crypto is shaky and the index worry hangs over it. **Risk:** Bitcoin is famously unpredictable and can jump overnight; if it rallies, this stock jumps even harder. This is a fast trade, not a hold — cap your risk.

### 5. Truist Financial (ticker: TFC) — watch, possible buy on the dip
- **Move:** Down about **2.5%** in pre-market after its quarterly report, though it was choppy.
- **Why:** The bank actually beat on profit ($1.16 per share vs. $1.14 expected), but its sales came in light — mostly because of a big one-time loss on selling some investments and a loan portfolio. The everyday banking business is fine; the miss was a cleanup item, not a broken engine.
- **What to do:** **Watch it today** and consider **buying** if it stops falling and steadies — the market often forgives a one-time accounting hit once it realizes it won't repeat. **Risk:** if higher interest rates slow down lending and the economy, that's a real, longer-lasting worry for all banks, so don't treat this as a sure thing.
