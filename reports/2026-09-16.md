## The big picture this morning (read this first)

Today is a huge day. At 2:00 in the afternoon (New York time) the Federal Reserve — the group that sets the country's interest rates — announces its decision, and Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh explains it 30 minutes later. Almost everyone expects the Fed to *raise* interest rates today (betting markets put the odds around 92%), which would be the **first rate hike in about three years**. Raising rates makes borrowing more expensive and is generally bad for expensive technology and fast-growing companies. Because this decision can swing the whole market up or down within minutes, most big investors are being careful and waiting until 2:00.

A few other forces are pushing prices around this morning:

1. **Government bond interest rates are the highest since 2007.** The interest rate on the 10-year U.S. government bond climbed above 5%. When you can earn 5% safely from a government bond, expensive tech stocks look less attractive by comparison, so money keeps leaking out of them.

2. **The "AI worry trade" is still on.** After the CEOs of the AI companies Anthropic and OpenAI warned that the race to build ever-more-powerful AI should slow down, traders got nervous that the enormous spending on AI computer chips might cool off. So money is flowing *out* of chip makers (Nvidia, AMD, Intel, Broadcom all fell recently, with AMD down about 6%) and *into* cybersecurity companies (the firms that keep computer systems safe, like CrowdStrike, Zscaler, and Palo Alto).

3. **Oil is still expensive** after a Saudi pipeline shutdown and Middle East tensions. That helps oil-company stocks but hurts almost everyone else.

4. **Bitcoin is weak, around $76,000** — still stuck below $80,000. Crypto funds lost about $592 million yesterday, partly because a crypto law (the CLARITY Act) failed to move forward in the U.S. Senate. Crypto-linked stocks are under pressure.

Bottom line: stock futures are barely moving before the opening bell because everyone is frozen in place waiting for the Fed at 2:00. Money keeps drifting *out* of expensive tech and chips and *into* oil, cybersecurity, and safer corners. **If you are not comfortable with sharp, sudden moves, it is completely fine to do nothing until after the Fed speaks this afternoon. Cash is a position too.**

## Top 5 Picks for Today

**1. Baldwin Group (ticker: BWIN) — trading near $32, being bought out for $32.50 in cash. A calm, low-drama pick for a nervous, Fed-decision day.**
Baldwin is an insurance company that just agreed to be taken private — meaning a group of investors (backed by the Dell family office) will buy the whole company for **$32.50 per share in cash**, an 88% jump over where it traded before the news. Here's why this is interesting on a scary day: once a cash buyout price is set, the stock usually just drifts slowly toward that price and barely reacts to whatever the Fed does. So if you can buy it a little below $32.50, you may collect the small remaining gap when the deal closes. **The catch:** the deal isn't expected to close until early 2027, so the gain is small and you'd be waiting a while, and there is always a slim chance a buyout falls apart. This is the "boring and steady" choice, not a home run.

**2. CrowdStrike (ticker: CRWD) — around $232 after a big jump. Consider buying, but you're paying up and the Fed is a wildcard.**
CrowdStrike is a cybersecurity company — it protects computer systems from hackers. It's the biggest winner of the "AI worry trade": with everyone suddenly nervous that AI is moving too fast, money is pouring into companies that keep systems safe. If that story stays in the headlines, CrowdStrike and its peers (Zscaler, Palo Alto) can keep climbing. **The catch:** the stock already leaped about 12–15% recently, so you'd be buying after a big pop, and if the Fed spooks the whole market this afternoon, even winners get pulled down for a day or two. Keep the size small and don't chase it higher.

**3. Betting against computer chips (ticker: SOXS) — only for people who think the chip slide keeps going this week.**
SOXS is a fund built to go *up* when semiconductor (computer-chip) stocks go *down*, and it moves about three times as fast as the chip group, in the opposite direction. Chips have been hit hard by the "slow down AI" fears, and a rate hike this afternoon could keep the pressure on expensive tech. **Big warning:** these "3x" funds are turbo-charged and lose value quickly if you're wrong or if you hold them more than a few days. Chip stocks can also bounce hard on any good headline, which would burn this trade fast. This is a short-leash, few-days bet only — never buy-and-forget.

**4. Diamondback Energy (ticker: FANG) or the oil basket (ticker: XLE) — a way to profit from expensive oil.**
Oil is sitting high because of the Saudi pipeline shutdown and Middle East tensions, and oil producers make more money when oil is expensive. Diamondback is one of the strongest U.S. oil drillers. If you'd rather spread your bet across many energy companies instead of picking one, buy the **Energy Select Sector SPDR Fund (ticker: XLE)**, a basket of big oil and gas stocks. Energy also tends to hold up better than tech when interest rates rise. **The risk:** if the Middle East calms down, oil can fall back just as fast, and these stocks would drop with it. This is a "ride it while the news is hot" trade.

**5. Betting against crypto — MicroStrategy / Strategy (ticker: MSTR). This could be one to bet against, or simply avoid, until the mood improves.**
MicroStrategy owns a giant pile of Bitcoin, so its stock swings with Bitcoin's price — usually even harder. With Bitcoin weak around $76,000, crypto funds bleeding money, and a crypto law stalling in the Senate, crypto-linked stocks are under real pressure, and a rate hike this afternoon would make risky bets even less popular. If the cautious mood holds, this can keep sliding. **The catch:** crypto stocks are extremely jumpy and can snap back fast on any good Bitcoin headline, so if you bet against it, keep it small and short. The calmer choice is simply to *avoid* crypto-linked names until after the Fed speaks.
