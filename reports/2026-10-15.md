## The big picture this morning (read this first)

Two things are driving almost everything today:

1. **Inflation came in hotter than expected.** A fresh report on rising prices landed this week, and it spooked people. Instead of cutting interest rates, the Federal Reserve (the U.S. central bank that sets interest rates) is now more likely to *raise* them again at its meeting this month. One big bank (UBS) even thinks there could be a third rate increase. Higher interest rates are bad for expensive, fast-growing tech stocks because they make borrowing money more costly — and a lot of the artificial-intelligence (AI) boom is being paid for with borrowed money. Another inflation report (this one on prices charged by factories and wholesalers) is due out today, and traders will react hard to it.

2. **Computer-chip stocks are falling sharply.** The main group of chip stocks dropped about 4% in a single day and is down roughly 12% over the past week. Nvidia, the biggest AI-chip maker, is down about 3% and sits around 7.5% below its record high from earlier this month. People are also starting to worry that AI companies are lending each other money in a big circle to buy each other's chips, which makes the whole boom look shakier.

So the mood is nervous and "risk-off" — meaning people are selling the riskiest, most expensive stocks and hiding in safer ones. Keep that backdrop in mind for everything below.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

Ericsson, the Swedish telecom-equipment company, jumped about **11%** before the market opened after reporting much better quarterly results than expected (both sales and profit beat forecasts). This is a rare bright spot on a gloomy morning — a real reason tied to real numbers, not just hype. When a stock pops on genuinely good earnings, it often keeps drifting up for a few days as more buyers pile in. **Plan in plain words:** this is one of the better "buy if the strength holds" ideas today. If it opens strong and stays green, it could be worth riding. If it fades back down near where it closed yesterday, that's your sign the pop is fizzling.

### 2. Taiwan Semiconductor (ticker: TSM) — be careful; it could keep sliding
This is the world's biggest maker of advanced chips, and it just reported *fantastic* numbers — profit up 69% from a year ago, beating expectations. Normally that sends a stock up. Instead it fell about **2%** before the open. Why? Two reasons: its profit margins slipped a little, and it warned it will spend a huge amount of money building new factories over the next few years. On top of that, the whole chip group is being dumped because of the interest-rate fears above. **Plan in plain words:** even great news isn't enough to lift chip stocks right now. Trying to buy TSM here is like trying to catch a falling knife — it could keep dropping with the rest of the group. If you already like it for the long run, fine, but for a quick 1–14 day trade this is a "wait until the chip selling calms down" name, not a "buy today" name.

### 3. Walmart (ticker: WMT) — consider buying as the safer choice
On a scary day, money often runs to boring, dependable companies, and they don't get more dependable than Walmart. It rose more than **2%** before the open after an analyst at Deutsche Bank raised its rating to "buy." A big store that sells cheap groceries actually does *better* when shoppers get nervous about prices, so Walmart fits today's worried mood. **Plan in plain words:** if you want to be in the market but hate the wild swings in tech, this is the kind of steady stock that tends to hold up while chips and AI names fall. A reasonable "consider buying" for the next couple of weeks.

### 4. Rocket Lab (ticker: RKLB) — this one could keep falling
Rocket Lab builds small rockets, and its stock is getting hit hard. The company pushed back the first launch of its bigger new rocket, called Neutron, into 2027 — later than people hoped. The stock is already down roughly 58% from its highs earlier this year, and the whole space sector got knocked down after SpaceX went public. **Plan in plain words:** there's no good near-term news here, only delays, and the trend is clearly down. This could be a candidate to bet against (make money if it keeps dropping) — but betting against a stock that's already fallen a lot is dangerous, because any small piece of good news can make it snap back up fast. Safest move for most people: simply avoid it until there's a real reason to own it.

### 5. Nvidia (ticker: NVDA) — the stock that's steering the whole market
Nvidia is the single most important stock for the mood right now. It's down about 3% and leads the chip group that's falling on interest-rate fears and the worry about AI companies borrowing too much. **Plan in plain words:** the short-term direction is down, so trying to "buy the dip" here is risky — it may dip further. For aggressive traders there are special funds that rise when chips fall (see SOXS in the "Also Watching" list), but those are extremely jumpy and can wipe out gains in hours. For most people, watching Nvidia tells you which way the market is leaning today: if Nvidia steadies and turns up, the whole market will likely feel calmer; if it keeps dropping, expect more red everywhere.
