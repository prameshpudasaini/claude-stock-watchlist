## First, the big picture (read this before anything else)

Two things make today unusually jumpy, so keep your position sizes small and be ready for sudden swings — especially in the final hour of trading (3–4 p.m. New York time):

1. **Today is a "quadruple witching" day.** Four types of stock and market contracts all expire at the same time today (this happens only four Fridays a year). In plain terms: a huge number of bets that traders placed earlier all come due at once. This creates much heavier trading than normal — often two to three times the usual volume — and can push prices around for reasons that have nothing to do with the actual companies. Expect extra choppiness, especially near the close.

2. **The Federal Reserve raised interest rates on Wednesday** — its first rate increase in three years, aimed at cooling stubborn inflation. Longer-term government bond interest rates (Treasury yields) briefly topped 5% for the first time since 2007. Higher rates generally hurt expensive, fast-growing tech and "story" stocks the most, and help nothing that depends on cheap borrowing. The market has been shaky about how many more hikes might come.

There's also a running debate this week about whether the AI building boom is slowing down. That pushed money out of chip makers (Nvidia, Intel, Micron) earlier in the week and into the big "cloud" companies (Meta, Microsoft, Alphabet) that could actually spend *less* on AI and still profit. Chips bounced back a bit on Thursday. Meanwhile, Bitcoin broke out to the upside (trading near $78,000), lighting a fire under crypto-related stocks.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

**1. Coinbase (COIN) — the main U.S. crypto exchange**
Pre-market read: around a strong up move (jumped roughly 18% on the crypto rebound). Bitcoin punched out of the price range it had been stuck in all year and is trading near $78,000, and Coinbase makes more money when crypto trading heats up. **What to consider:** if Bitcoin keeps climbing, buying Coinbase is a way to ride that wave with a stock. It moves much more violently than Bitcoin itself, so it can jump fast — and drop just as fast. If Bitcoin stalls or turns down, get out; don't marry this one.

**2. Strategy (MSTR) — a company that mostly holds Bitcoin**
Pre-market read: up roughly 10%. This company (formerly MicroStrategy) owns a giant pile of Bitcoin, so its stock is basically a turbo-charged bet on Bitcoin's price. **What to consider:** same idea as Coinbase — a way to bet on Bitcoin continuing higher, but with even bigger swings. Only worth it while Bitcoin is clearly going up. The moment Bitcoin rolls over, this one tends to fall harder than the coin.

**3. Intel (INTC) — U.S. computer chip maker**
Pre-market read: around $110, up about 1%. News that memory-chip maker SK Hynix is in talks with Intel is boosting sentiment, and the stock is pressing against a price ceiling it has bumped into before. **What to consider:** a possible buy *if* it keeps pushing higher through that ceiling on strong volume. Be careful — the whole chip group is caught in the "is AI spending slowing?" argument, so Intel can get dragged down by the group even on its own good news.

**4. Fluence Energy (FLNC) — big-battery/energy-storage company — CAUTION / AVOID**
Pre-market read: down about 22%. The company slashed its own sales forecast for the year (now expecting about $2.4 billion instead of $2.9–3.1 billion) and guided to a sizable loss. **What to consider:** this is a warning, not a bargain. A cut this big often means more bad news is coming, and trying to catch a falling stock right after a guidance cut is risky. Better to leave it alone today, or only bet against it (bet it keeps falling) if it can't hold this morning's lows. Don't rush to "buy the discount."

**5. Meta Platforms (META) or Microsoft (MSFT) — the "AI winners if spending slows" trade**
Pre-market read: firmer than the chip makers. The new story on Wall Street is that if AI companies pull back on spending, the chip makers lose but the big cloud/software companies could actually make *more* profit (they'd spend less and still charge for AI services). Money has been rotating their way. **What to consider:** a steadier, lower-drama place to be than chips right now. Consider buying if they keep outperforming the chip names. These are large, stable companies, so don't expect fireworks — this is the calmer pick on the list.
