# 2026-07-14 — Daily Trading Watchlist

> **Tuesday, the double-catalyst day arrived.** Two big things hit at 8:30 a.m. ET together: (1) **June CPI came in much cooler than expected** — headline **-0.4% m/m** (biggest monthly drop in 6+ years), annual rate down to **3.5%** from 4.2%, core **flat** at **2.6% y/y** — and (2) **five megabanks reported Q2 earnings**, mostly big beats. But the tape is NOT a clean rip: **IBM warned on preliminary results and is down ~23%**, dragging software, and Middle East tensions are simmering. Futures are little changed to mixed. This is a stock-picker's day, not an index day.

---

## The one thing driving everything: cool CPI + bank beats vs. an IBM software bomb

The inflation scare cooled hard — energy fell 5.7%, headline printed negative, core was flat. Normally a green-light for risk. **The catch: the Fed is still expected to HOLD July 28-29 and then HIKE a quarter-point in September** (Waller wants "several months" of good data), so bonds rallied (yields sharply lower) but stocks didn't get a rate-cut party. Meanwhile the banks largely delivered — **JPM and Goldman crushed it** — yet the reactions are muted because a **23% IBM collapse** is spraying shrapnel across software (MSFT, CRM, WDAY, ORCL all lower). The tell inside the IBM miss is bullish for one group: clients redirected spend toward **memory and servers ahead of price hikes** — the same supply-constrained memory story that's been powering chips. So today splits cleanly: **long financials and memory/semis, avoid legacy software.**

## Top 5 Picks for Today

**1. Goldman Sachs — GS — Long**
Goldman posted a monster quarter: **EPS $20.98 vs $14.48 expected**, revenue $20.34B vs $16.13B — and shares rose **+1.4%** premarket despite the messy tape. Options had priced the widest implied move of the banks (~6%), so there's room to run as the print gets digested. Trading/IB rebound is real. Play: long the best-in-class beat; add on any dip that holds. Risk: if the broad tape rolls over on IBM/geopolitics, even a great bank print can fade intraday.

**2. IBM — IBM — Watch for short / avoid the falling knife**
IBM warned preliminary Q2 came in light (**EPS $2.93 vs $3.01, rev $17.2B vs $17.86B**) and cratered **~23%** — its worst day in decades. Software peers got dragged (**WDAY -10%, CRM -6.2%, MSFT -3%, ORCL -2.1%, ADSK -5%**). Play: this is a momentum-short/read-through trade, not a dip-buy — legacy software weakness plus a "clients spending on hardware not software" narrative is a multi-day story. Risk: 23% down days often see a violent oversold bounce; don't chase the short late, and mind a possible relief pop.

**3. Memory & Semis (MU / SOXL / TSEM) — Long**
The bullish nugget buried in IBM's miss: customers are pulling forward **memory/server capex to lock supply ahead of price increases.** That's rocket fuel for the memory complex. Semis are already bouncing — **AMAT +5.3%, Teradyne +4.9%, MPWR +4.5%**, and **Tower Semiconductor (TSEM)** popped on a **$3B Japan expansion** (with $1B in grants) for AI/data-center chips. Play: long memory (MU) or SOXL for the bounce; TSEM for the fresh contract catalyst. Risk: chips have been a $1T two-way wrecking ball this month — leveraged SOXL is a 1-3 day trade, not a hold.

**4. JPMorgan Chase — JPM — Long / core financial**
JPM delivered **one of its biggest beats ever: EPS $6.14 vs $5.85, revenue $58.02B vs $50.19B.** The cool CPI removes the "hot inflation kills the tape" risk that was the main threat into today. Financials are the clean, catalyst-backed long of the session. Play: long the group leader; use BAC (beat, flat) as a cheaper laggard catch-up. Risk: banks have run up into the print, so "sell-the-news" profit-taking is possible even on good numbers (see WFC below).

**5. Q32 Bio — QTTB — Momentum long / watch**
Q32 reported **strong 36-week Phase 2a alopecia areata data** for bempikibart (35.3% mean SALT reduction, one patient full regrowth, clean safety) and the stock **~doubled to ~$20**. It also launched a **$200M offering** to capitalize on the move. Play: speculative momentum long on orderly consolidation; the data is a genuine de-risking event. Risk: it already ran 80-100%, and the raise adds supply — expect sharp two-way chop, keep size small.
