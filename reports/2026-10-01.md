# Short-Term Stock Watchlist — Thursday, October 1, 2026

*A plain-English guide to today's best short-term trading ideas (hold roughly 1 to 14 days). This is research and opinion, not financial advice. Short-term trading is risky — never bet money you can't afford to lose.*

## What's driving the whole market today

It's the first day of a brand-new month (and a new three-month stretch, October through December), and money is flowing back into the artificial-intelligence (AI) and computer-chip stocks that have led the market all year. Before the opening bell, the tech-heavy Nasdaq index was pointing up about **1%** and the broad S&P 500 about **0.6%**, while the Dow was mixed.

Two pieces of good news are lighting the fire under tech:
1. **Micron's blowout results from last night are still echoing.** Micron, a big memory-chip maker, said demand for its AI chips is enormous, and that excitement is lifting the whole chip group again this morning.
2. **Google's parent, Alphabet, launched its most powerful AI model yet** (called Gemini 4 Argon). Its stock rose about 1.5%, and the news adds to the "AI is still booming" mood.

But there's a real tug-of-war going on. On the other side is a worry that won't go away: the interest rate on 10-year U.S. government bonds has climbed to its **highest level in more than 20 years** (since 2002), and the U.S. dollar is strong. When these government-bond interest rates (called "yields") go up, it makes expensive stocks — especially tech — harder to justify, because investors can earn more by just parking money in safe bonds. Oil prices are also creeping higher.

One worry that got taken *off* the table: there is **no government shutdown** today even though it's the start of the government's new budget year. Congress passed a short-term funding bill that keeps the government running through December 11, so that's one less thing to fret about this morning.

The simple takeaway: the AI and chip excitement is strong enough to push the market up today, but those high bond interest rates are a storm cloud on the horizon. Stick to stocks that have their own clear, fresh news — and keep in mind that if those bond rates keep climbing, the most expensive tech names are the ones that get hit first.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

### 1. Nebius Group (NBIS) — consider buying
Nebius rents out powerful computers (loaded with Nvidia AI chips) that other companies use to build artificial intelligence. Today it did two things that traders love: it **raised the prices** it charges for that computing power — starting today — by roughly 17% to 25%, and a big bank (BNP Paribas) upgraded the stock to its "buy" category and lifted its price target all the way to **$399** (from $260). The stock rose about **9%** this morning (it was up as much as 11% earlier). **Consider buying** — a company raising prices and still having customers lined up is a sign of real, strong demand, and this is a direct way to bet on the AI computing boom. **The risk:** this stock swings hard, and it's an expensive, fast-growing name — exactly the kind that falls fastest if those high bond interest rates spook the market.

### 2. United Therapeutics (UTHR) — consider buying
This is the clear winner from a big court fight. A court ruled that a rival drug company, Liquidia, **violated United Therapeutics' patent** on an inhaled lung-disease treatment. In plain words: a competitor just got blocked from fully selling a product that would have eaten into United's sales. United's stock jumped about **12%** on the news. **Consider buying** — removing a competitor is a solid, real reason for the stock to move, and United is an established, profitable company (not a risky small one). **The risk:** Liquidia says it will appeal, so there's a small chance the court decision gets softened later; also the stock already popped, so some of the easy gain may be done.

### 3. Rocket Lab (RKLB) — consider buying
Rocket Lab launches small rockets into space for other companies. Today it announced its **biggest commercial launch deal ever** for its Electron rocket — a multi-year agreement to fly 20 missions for a Japanese satellite company called Synspective. The stock rose about **4.8%**. **Consider buying** — a large, multi-year contract means years of booked-in work and shows the business is growing, and space and defense stocks have been popular all year. **The risk:** Rocket Lab is still an unprofitable, higher-risk company whose stock moves a lot, and rockets occasionally fail — any launch problem could knock the shares down quickly.

### 4. American Battery Technology (ABAT) — consider buying (higher risk)
This is a small company that recycles old batteries to pull out valuable metals. Today the U.S. government gave it approval to **export up to $100 million** of recycled battery material. The stock rose about **10%** before the bell. **Consider buying** for those comfortable with risk — government approval like this opens a real new source of money for a small company, and battery/clean-energy names can run hard on this kind of news. **The risk:** this is a small, jumpy company that doesn't make steady profits yet, so the pop could fade fast; keep any bet small.

### 5. Micron Technology (MU) — consider buying
Micron makes the memory chips that AI computers need, and last night it reported huge demand with its most advanced chips sold out far into the future. That news is still lifting the stock and the whole chip group today, including giants like Nvidia (NVDA). **Consider buying** — this is the clearest signal that the AI chip boom is still going strong, and when Micron leads, other chip names usually follow. **The risk:** the stock already leaped on the results, so it could give back some of that jump in a day or two — don't chase it if it spikes hard at the open, and remember expensive chip stocks fall fastest if those high bond interest rates rattle the market.

## Big Movers (up or down about 40% or more today)

- **Liquidia (LQDA) — down about 57%.** It lost the patent court fight described in pick #2, so a court says it can't fully sell its inhaled lung-disease drug; it will ask the FDA to change the drug's label and plans to appeal. **Risk warning:** after a crash this big, a stock can bounce back for a day or two on bargain-hunting even when the news is genuinely bad — so betting it keeps falling is risky, and any hopeful appeal headline could pop it higher.
- **GlucoTrack (GCTK) — up roughly 107% to 120%+.** This tiny medical-device company spiked after announcing a deal to **sell up to $20 million of new stock**. **Risk warning:** selling lots of new shares actually divides the company into more pieces (which normally *hurts* each share), so this kind of spike is really just excited short-term traders — it can crash back down just as fast. Treat it as gambling, not investing.
- **Stardust Power (SDST) — up about 109% to $0.33.** A penny stock (trades for pennies) that jumped on heavy, speculative trading. **Risk warning:** stocks this tiny and this cheap are extremely easy to push up and down; there's no durable news here, and these routinely give back 100% moves within days.
- **CID HoldCo (DAIC) — up about 96% to $3.95.** Another very small stock making an outsized move on light news. **Risk warning:** same as above — tiny, thinly-traded names like this can reverse violently, so this is a gamble, not a sound bet.
- **Origin Materials (ORGN) — down about 43%.** This maker of plant-based materials fell hard on news of **big cost overruns and delays** at its next factory, plus worries about how it will pay for them. **Risk warning:** when a small company's costs balloon and it may need to raise cash, the stock can keep sliding — don't try to catch this falling knife.
- **22nd Century Group (XXII) — down about 39%.** A tiny tobacco-technology company that keeps falling on money-losing results; its total value is now extremely small. **Risk warning:** this is a micro-sized, barely-traded stock near the edge — avoid it entirely.

(General note: the gainers above that are tiny stocks spiking 90%–120% almost always have a shaky reason — a stock sale, a vague press release, or just trader hype. They can crash as fast as they jump. None are recommended here; they're flagged only so you know why they're on the "biggest movers" screens.)

### Quick reminder on risk
Today's mood is upbeat because the AI and chip excitement (Micron, Google, Nebius) is strong — but don't forget the storm cloud: government-bond interest rates are at a 20-plus-year high, and if they keep climbing, the expensive tech stocks leading today are the first to get hit. Stick to stocks with their own clear, fresh news (like United Therapeutics' patent win, Nebius' price hikes, and Rocket Lab's new contract), stay far away from the tiny penny stocks spiking on hype, keep every position small, and decide the price at which you'll sell *before* you buy.
