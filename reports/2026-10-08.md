# Short-Term Stock Watchlist — Thursday, October 8, 2026

*A plain-English guide to today's best short-term trading ideas (hold roughly 1 to 14 days). This is research and opinion, not financial advice. Short-term trading is risky — never bet money you can't afford to lose.*

## What's driving the whole market today

The mood this Thursday is nervous, and the reason is simple: **oil prices jumped**. Before the opening bell, U.S. stock futures (bets on where the market opens) were pointing **lower** — Dow futures down about 0.9%, the S&P 500 fund down about 0.5%, and the Nasdaq-100 fund down about 0.7% — after the market slipped from record highs the day before.

Here's the backdrop in plain words:

1. **Oil is the big story.** Crude oil shot up around 5% this morning, with the global benchmark (Brent) back above $100 a barrel, after peace talks between the United States and Iran stalled and tensions in the Middle East flared up again. Higher oil means higher gasoline, shipping, and fuel costs for everyone — which makes people worry about **inflation** (prices rising) coming back. When energy prices spike like this, money tends to flow *into* oil and gas companies and *out of* everything that gets hurt by expensive fuel, like airlines.

2. **Borrowing rates are the highest in decades.** The U.S. government's 10-year borrowing rate (the "10-year Treasury yield," a key number the whole market watches) climbed back toward **5.35%** — its highest since 2002. High rates make borrowing pricier for companies and make safe bonds more tempting than stocks. This is an extra weight on expensive, fast-growing tech stocks.

3. **The feared mix: slow growth plus rising prices.** Pricey oil on top of already-high interest rates has traders worried about "stagflation" — a sluggish economy and rising prices at the same time, which is an uncomfortable combination for stocks.

4. **Earnings season is just starting.** The big quarterly report season kicks off this week. PepsiCo reported this morning, and the airline Delta reports tomorrow morning — and with jet fuel now more expensive, all eyes are on how airlines are coping.

The simple takeaway: the market is near record highs but pulling back today, and the swing factor is oil and interest rates. Don't chase anything blindly. The clearest opportunities today are in energy (helped by the oil jump) and in a handful of companies with their own fresh news. Keep every bet small.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

### 1. Energy / oil-and-gas stocks (watch XLE, OXY, XOM) — consider buying
With oil up about 5% this morning, the companies that pump and sell oil are the day's most obvious winners. The simplest way to play it is the **Energy Select Sector fund (XLE)**, a basket of big U.S. energy companies, or individual names like **Occidental Petroleum (OXY)** and **ExxonMobil (XOM)**. **Consider buying** — when oil spikes on a supply scare (here, the breakdown in U.S.–Iran talks), energy stocks usually ride the move for several days, and this is the one corner of the market working *with* today's news instead of against it. **The risk:** oil moves on headlines, and headlines flip fast. If the Middle East tension cools or the two sides return to the table, oil could drop just as quickly and take these stocks back down. This is a bet on the news staying hot — keep it small and set a price where you'll sell if it reverses.

### 2. Wolfspeed (WOLF) — consider buying (higher risk)
Wolfspeed makes a special kind of computer chip (silicon carbide) used in electric cars, power systems, and defense gear. Before the bell the stock jumped roughly **15% to 25%** after the company said the U.S. Department of Defense gave it a **conditional commitment for up to $1.5 billion in long-term (30-year) financing**. **Consider buying** — this is a big vote of confidence from the government in a company that has badly needed cash, and news like this can keep pulling in buyers for a few days. **The risk — and it's a serious one:** the money is only a *conditional* commitment, not a done deal, and still depends on negotiations and conditions being met. Wolfspeed has a troubled recent history (it has warned in the past about whether it could keep operating) and the stock has crashed hard before. Treat this as a high-risk trade on the excitement, not a safe long-term bet.

### 3. Haemonetics (HAE) — consider buying
Haemonetics makes medical equipment used to collect blood and plasma. The stock rose about **8%** before the bell after its big partner, CSL, said it expects to finish rolling out Haemonetics' plasma-collection system across the United States by the end of 2027. **Consider buying** — this is good, concrete news (a major customer confirming it's expanding its use of the company's product), and it's in healthcare, a calmer corner of the market that isn't getting hurt by today's oil-and-rates worries. **The risk:** the rollout runs through 2027, so this is more of a steady, slower story than an explosive one — don't expect a huge additional pop, and a weak overall market day could still drag it down a bit.

### 4. Palantir (PLTR) — consider buying (higher risk)
Palantir makes data-analysis and artificial-intelligence software used by governments and big companies. The stock rose about **2.3%** before the bell after well-known technology analyst Dan Ives — now at his own new firm — started covering it with a positive ("outperform") rating and a high price target (reported around $250). **Consider buying** — Palantir is one of the most popular AI stocks with everyday investors, and a fresh thumbs-up from a famous analyst often brings in more buyers over the next few days. **The risk:** Palantir is extremely expensive relative to its earnings, and pricey AI stocks are exactly what gets hit hardest when interest rates rise — which they're doing today. It swings a lot in both directions, so keep the position small.

### 5. PepsiCo (PEP) — consider buying (with caution)
PepsiCo makes soft drinks and snacks (Pepsi, Gatorade, Frito-Lay). This morning it reported quarterly results that **beat expectations** on both sales and profit, and the stock rose about **2%** before the bell. **Consider buying** — Pepsi is a steady, defensive company (people keep buying snacks and drinks even in a shaky economy), which makes it a calmer place to be on a nervous day like today, and a solid earnings report can give it a lift for several days. **The risk:** alongside the beat, Pepsi actually *lowered* its profit forecast for the full year (now expecting earnings to grow only about 2.5%–3.5%, down from a higher prior guess), saying its North American turnaround is taking longer than hoped. That's a yellow flag — the early pop could fade if investors focus on the weaker outlook, so don't chase it if it runs up hard.

Risk: the mixed quality of the report makes this one to observe rather than rush into.
5. **The leveraged market funds (SQQQ up, TQQQ down) — higher-risk, short-term only.** These are turbo-charged funds that amplify the market's daily move. With tech futures pointing down today, the fund that rises when the Nasdaq falls (**SQQQ**) tends to climb on days like this, while the one that rises when the Nasdaq climbs (**TQQQ**) tends to drop. Risk: these are built for a single day's trade and lose value if you hold them too long — only for experienced traders watching closely, never a buy-and-forget.
