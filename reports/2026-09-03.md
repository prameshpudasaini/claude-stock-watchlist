## The big picture this morning

Yesterday the market snapped a three-day slump. All three main U.S. stock gauges finished higher on Wednesday — the Dow closed at 53,061.95 (up about 0.6%), the S&P 500 at 7,666.60 (up about 0.5%), and the Nasdaq at 26,217.83 (up about 0.5%). This morning stock futures are roughly flat to slightly higher, so the mood is cautious but steady.

Two things are driving the whole market right now:

- **The job market is looking soft, and that's actually helping stocks.** A widely-watched private hiring report (from ADP) showed U.S. companies added just 38,000 jobs in August, fewer than the roughly 47,000 economists expected. Weak hiring makes investors believe the Federal Reserve is more likely to cut interest rates, and lower rates tend to lift stock prices.
- **The really big event is tomorrow (Friday).** The government's official August jobs report comes out Friday morning (economists expect around 45,000 new jobs). This is the single most important number of the week. A weak number could push stocks up (more hope for a rate cut); a surprisingly strong number could knock them down. Expect choppy trading into that report, and know that anything you hold overnight into Friday carries that risk.

One warning sign in the background: the interest rate on the 10-year U.S. government bond is sitting near 4.8%, which is high. That keeps borrowing expensive for everyone and can put a lid on how far stocks run.

If it holds its gains today and keeps climbing, it's a reasonable short-term momentum buy — but same caution: you're buying after a big one-day jump.

Nvidia moves the whole market, and a fresh piece of news like this can give it a multi-day lift. A more modest, sensible pick than the two big earnings jumpers above because the move is smaller and the company is rock-solid.

**4. ChargePoint (CHPT) — up about 17% after beating earnings. A higher-risk turnaround bet.**
ChargePoint builds electric-vehicle charging stations. It beat expectations, grew revenue almost 18% from a year ago, and improved its profit margins. Higher risk, higher reward — only worth it if you're comfortable with a bumpy ride, and keep the position small.

**5. Broadcom (AVGO) — down about 2.5% on a disappointing forecast. This could be one to bet against if it keeps sliding, or a bounce buy if it steadies.**
Broadcom is a major chip company tied to the AI boom. Its results were fine, but its revenue forecast for next quarter ($34.8 billion) came in just below what Wall Street wanted ($35.03 billion), and investors punished it. Two ways to play it: if the stock keeps falling today, it could keep drifting lower for a few days as disappointed holders sell — a possible bet-against. But if it steadies near where it is, some traders will treat the dip as a chance to buy a strong company cheaper. Watch which way it leans in the first hour of trading before deciding.
