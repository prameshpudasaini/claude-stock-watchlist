## The one big thing driving everything today

This morning the government released the September jobs report (how many new
jobs U.S. employers added last month), and it was much weaker than expected.
Employers added only **29,000 jobs**, far below the roughly **84,000** that
experts had predicted, and the unemployment rate ticked up to **4.2%**.

Why this matters for you: a weak jobs market makes it much more likely the
Federal Reserve (the U.S. central bank that sets interest rates) will cut
interest rates soon. Lower interest rates are generally good for stocks,
especially fast-growing technology and smaller companies. So even though the
economic news sounds bad, the stock market liked it — stock index futures
(bets on where the market will open) jumped, with the Dow, S&P 500, and
Nasdaq all pointing up about 0.8% to 1%. Interest rates on government bonds
fell. Chip and technology stocks are leading the way up.

**Plain-English takeaway:** Today is shaping up to be an "up" day driven by
hopes for lower interest rates. That tends to help technology, chips, and
smaller companies the most.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

### 1. Accenture (ACN) — consider buying
**Pre-market move:** up about 17–20% (a huge one-day jump for such a large
company).
Accenture is a giant consulting and technology-services company. It just
reported quarterly results that beat expectations (earnings of $3.94 per
share versus the $3.74 that experts predicted, and revenue of $18.7 billion),
and it gave an upbeat forecast. A big reason is strong demand for its help
with artificial intelligence (AI) — it booked $2.2 billion of new AI work in
the quarter. **Why watch it:** a clear, fresh piece of good news is pushing a
large, stable company sharply higher. When a big company like this beats and
raises its outlook, the move often has staying power for several days.
Consider buying if it holds its gains, but don't chase it if it has already
run up a lot by the time you look — a 20% pop can give some back.

### 2. Nike (NKE) — consider betting against it (or just stay away)
**Pre-market move:** down about 10%, to roughly $31.79. This is the second day
in a row it has fallen.
Nike reported falling sales and said it plans to lay off staff in 2027, and
its forecast for the year ahead disappointed investors. **Why watch it:** the
news is clearly bad and the stock is dropping two days running, which suggests
sellers are in control for now. This could be one to bet against if it keeps
sliding, or simply a name to avoid buying today — "catching" a falling stock
hoping for a quick bounce is risky when the company's own outlook is weak.

### 3. Synaptics (SYNA) — lower-risk buy tied to a buyout
**Pre-market move:** up about 14%.
Another chip company, onsemi, has agreed to buy Synaptics, and today they
changed the deal to an all-cash offer of **$123 per share**. **Why watch it:**
when a company is being bought for cash at a set price, its stock usually
trades close to that price and is far less jumpy than a normal stock. This is
a calmer, lower-risk way to play today's news — the main thing that could go
wrong is the deal falling apart or taking a long time to close (it is expected
to close in 2027). Consider buying if it is trading meaningfully below $123,
understanding the gain is modest and the wait could be long.

### 4. American Battery Technology Company (ABAT) — consider buying, but it's volatile
**Pre-market move:** up about 17% on unusually heavy trading.
This small company recycles batteries and produces battery metals. It just got
U.S. Department of Commerce approval tied to about $100 million of mineral
exports. **Why watch it:** a concrete piece of government-related good news is
driving heavy buying, and battery/critical-minerals names have been a hot
theme. Consider buying if the strong volume continues, but keep positions
small — small companies like this swing hard in both directions.

### 5. A chip "basket" to ride the rate-cut tailwind — consider buying, e.g. Micron (MU) or the SOXL fund
**Why today:** with the weak jobs report boosting hopes for lower interest
rates, money is flowing into chip stocks — the main chip fund (ticker SMH) is
up over 1% and Micron is trading higher on heavy volume. Big Wall Street firm
Bank of America just raised its forecast for the whole chip industry to a
record $1.3 trillion in sales for 2026 and named Nvidia (NVDA), Broadcom
(AVGO), Marvell (MRVL), and AMD as top picks. **How to play it:** for a
steadier bet, consider a chipmaker like Micron (MU) or Nvidia (NVDA). For a
more aggressive, amplified bet on chips going up today, there is a fund called
SOXL that multiplies the daily move of chip stocks — but be careful: funds
like SOXL amplify losses just as much as gains and are only for very
short-term holding, not something to sit on for two weeks.

### Quick reminders before you trade
- Pre-market prices often look bigger than the real move; wait to see how a
  stock trades after the 9:30 a.m. ET open before committing.
- Small companies (anything under about $5, or with "tiny" in the warning
  above) can drop as fast as they rise. Use small amounts.
- Amplified/leveraged funds (like SOXL, SQQQ, TQQQ) are built for a single
  day's move, not for holding a week or two — they can quietly lose value even
  if you're right about the direction over time.
