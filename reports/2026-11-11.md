## What's going on in the market today

**Quick heads-up: today is Veterans Day.** The stock market (where you buy and sell shares) is **open and trading normally**. Only the bond market (where governments and companies borrow money) is closed. One small side effect is that fewer big professional traders are at their desks on a holiday, so prices can swing around a little more easily than usual on light trading.

Two things are driving the mood this morning, and both point *up*.

**First, the big technology stocks are bouncing back.** Yesterday the market had a rough day after an inflation report (the government's measure of how fast prices are rising) and worries about higher interest rates. This morning the mood flipped: the stocks of companies that make computer chips for artificial intelligence are rising again. Nvidia — the biggest name in AI chips — is up about **5%** after falling hard for two days (those two days wiped out more than $430 billion of its value). When Nvidia bounces, the whole chip group tends to follow: Micron up about 6.5%, AMD up about 6.4%, Intel up about 6.2%, and Broadcom up about 4.2% before the opening bell. The futures (early bets on where the market opens) point clearly higher.

**One cloud is still hanging over Nvidia, though.** Its CEO, Jensen Huang, is tangled in a scandal and has been called to answer questions in front of Congress next **Monday, November 18**. Nvidia has even hired a law firm to look into whether any company secrets leaked. So the bounce is real, but the drama isn't over — keep that date in mind before buying anything chip-related.

**Second, a big AI company reported good news overnight.** CoreWeave, which rents out computing power for artificial intelligence, reported its quarterly results after the market closed yesterday, beat expectations, and raised its forecast for the year. Its stock is up strongly this morning (more on that below). That's being read as a sign the AI spending boom is still going, which is helping lift other AI names.

The clearest fresh opportunities today are the chip bounce, a couple of earnings winners, and some wild small-company moves you should mostly admire from a safe distance.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

Just as important, the stock had already fallen about 40% over the past month, so a lot of nervous sellers were already gone; good news into a beaten-down stock can bounce harder. **This is a reasonable one to consider buying.** The catch: it has already jumped a lot before the market even opened, and AI stocks have been jumpy lately, so don't chase it in a panic. If you like it, decide in advance the price at which you'd admit you were wrong and step away.

Keep any bet small, and don't overpay chasing it after it has already run.

### 4. Mercury Systems (MRCY) — consider buying
This one has a clean, specific reason to move today: a major Wall Street firm (JPMorgan) just **upgraded the stock to its "buy" category and set a target price of $102**. Mercury Systems makes specialized electronics for defense and aerospace — the gear that goes inside military and space systems. When a big firm turns positive, other investors often follow over the next few days, which fits a short holding period. **A reasonable one to consider buying.** The risk is milder than the names above (no earnings bombshell looming), but a single analyst's opinion isn't a guarantee, so don't bet the farm, and check where the stock is actually trading before you jump in.

### 5. Microvast (MVST) — stay away; this one is in real trouble
Pre-market **down about 48%**, trading around **$0.22** (yes, that's 22 cents). Microvast makes batteries, and its latest results were ugly: sales came in far below expectations, and — most worrying — the company gave a "going concern" warning, which is accounting-speak for *we're not sure we can keep paying our bills*. It's scrambling to restructure its debt. **The safe move is to stay far away.** When a company is openly warning about survival and the stock is already a fraction of a dollar, it can keep falling toward zero. If you were ever tempted to bet against it (betting a stock will fall), the story is genuinely bad — but a stock this cheap can also violently snap upward for a day on any rescue rumor, which can burn anyone betting against it. For most people, the right call is simply: don't touch it.
