## The big picture this morning

Two things are driving the whole market today:

1. **Fighting between the U.S. and Iran flared up again over the weekend.** U.S. forces hit Iranian rocket launchers near the Strait of Hormuz (the narrow sea passage where a huge share of the world's oil ships through), and Iran hit back at U.S. bases. Because of this, the price of oil jumped about 3% this morning. That's good for oil and defense (weapons) companies, and it's making investors nervous about everything else.
2. **Stock futures are slightly lower** (down about 0.2%), and there's worry the Federal Reserve (the U.S. central bank that sets interest rates) may keep rates high or even raise them. Higher rates usually push stock prices down.

This is the last trading day of August. Two other things to keep in mind: it's Tim Cook's final day as CEO of Apple (John Ternus takes over tomorrow), and this is a big earnings week — chip giant **Broadcom** and computer maker **Dell** both report their quarterly results, and the government's monthly jobs report comes out Friday. Those events can move the whole market.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

### 1. Chevron (CVX) — consider buying
- **Price:** around $200, roughly flat to slightly lower in early trading, but the whole oil group is up.
- **Why watch it:** When oil prices jump like they did today (up about 3% on the Iran fighting), big oil companies usually make more money, and their stocks tend to rise. Chevron is one of the largest and safest ways to bet on higher oil. It has been a strong performer all year and sits near its highest price in 12 months.
- **What to do:** Consider buying if oil keeps climbing and the Middle East situation stays tense. The risk: if the fighting calms down quickly, oil could fall back and take these stocks with it. Other oil names moving up on the same news: Exxon Mobil (XOM), Occidental (OXY), ConocoPhillips (COP), and the oilfield service firms Halliburton (HAL) and SLB.

### 2. RTX Corporation (RTX) — consider buying
- **Price:** rising; the maker of Raytheon missiles and other weapons has been hitting new 12-month highs.
- **Why watch it:** When countries go to war or fear war, governments buy more weapons. The U.S. reportedly used up almost all of its long-range missiles during the recent conflict, so it will need to buy more — and RTX makes them. Lockheed Martin (LMT) and Northrop Grumman (NOC) are in the same boat and also near record highs.
- **What to do:** Consider buying while the conflict is in the headlines. The risk: defense stocks can drop fast if a ceasefire or peace deal appears, because the "war premium" comes out of the price quickly.

### 3. Broadcom (AVGO) — watch closely, high-reward but risky
- **Price:** climbing ahead of its earnings report.
- **Why watch it:** Broadcom reports its quarterly results **this Wednesday, September 2, after the market closes.** It's one of the biggest makers of custom chips for artificial intelligence (AI). Wall Street expects its AI chip sales to more than double from a year ago (to about $16 billion for the quarter). A strong report could lift not just Broadcom but the whole AI and chip group.
- **What to do:** Some people buy a few days before a report like this hoping for good news. But be careful: earnings reports are a coin-flip. If the numbers disappoint even a little, the stock can drop sharply the next morning — we just saw that happen to chipmaker Marvell (MRVL), which fell 7% after its report despite raising its forecasts. If you don't want that risk, it's safer to wait and watch how it reacts after Wednesday.

### 4. PG&E (PCG) — be very careful; this could keep falling
- **Price:** down about 13% to 16% today, one of the worst performers.
- **Why watch it:** California lawmakers passed a bill (SB 492) but left out the protection PG&E wanted, which would have shielded the utility from paying huge wildfire damage bills. Because that protection didn't happen, the company is now seen as riskier, and at least three Wall Street firms (Wells Fargo, Mizuho, and BMO) cut their ratings and price targets on the stock this morning.
- **What to do:** This is a falling knife — a stock dropping fast on bad news. It could be a candidate to bet against if it keeps sliding, but that's advanced and risky. For most people, the safer move is simply to stay away until the dust settles. Do NOT try to "catch the bottom" here; bad news like this can keep pushing a stock lower for days.

### 5. Coinbase (COIN) — consider buying if crypto stays hot
- **Price:** riding the crypto wave; Bitcoin recently pushed above $80,000 for the first time in about 15 weeks.
- **Why watch it:** Coinbase is the biggest U.S. crypto trading platform, and it makes more money when Bitcoin is rising and people are trading a lot. Bitcoin climbed more than 25% in about 10 days. Other ways to ride the same trend: Strategy (MSTR, formerly MicroStrategy), which owns a giant pile of Bitcoin, and miners like MARA Holdings (MARA).
- **What to do:** Consider buying if Bitcoin holds above $80,000 and keeps climbing. The risk: crypto is extremely jumpy. If Bitcoin turns down — especially if the Federal Reserve signals higher interest rates this week — these stocks can fall even faster than Bitcoin itself.

## Big Movers (up or down about 40% or more today)

- **Antelope Enterprise (AEHL) — up roughly 65% to 90%.** This tiny company jumped after announcing it raised about $19 million by selling new shares and warrants (a "private placement"). **Big warning:** raising money this way usually means existing shareholders get diluted (their slice of the company shrinks). Tiny stocks like this, with very few shares trading normally, can spike on a news headline and then crash back down just as fast, sometimes within the same day. Extremely risky — treat any position as money you could lose entirely.

- **RedHill Biopharma (RDHL) — reportedly up around 100%.** This is a very small, low-priced biotech stock (recently under $1 a share) that doubled on heavy trading volume. I could not confirm the exact piece of news behind the jump, which is itself a red flag. **Big warning:** penny-stock biotech names can double one day and give it all back the next. If you can't find a clear, solid reason for the move, the safest choice is to leave it alone.
