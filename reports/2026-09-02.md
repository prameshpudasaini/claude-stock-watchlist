# September 2, 2026 — Short-Term Stock Trading Report

*(For trades you plan to hold roughly 1 to 14 days. Plain-English notes, not financial advice. Prices and moves are from this morning's pre-market and early-session data and change fast — check a live quote before you act.)*

## The one big story driving everything today

Today is all about **oil and interest rates**, and both are pushing in the same scary direction.

- **Oil is spiking.** The fighting between the United States and Iran flared up again over the weekend — there were attacks on ships in the Strait of Hormuz (the narrow sea passage that about a fifth of the world's oil normally travels through). U.S. oil (WTI) is up about 2.4% this morning to roughly **$87.80 a barrel**, and the global price (Brent) is around **$91**.
- **Interest rates are climbing.** The interest rate on the 10-year U.S. government bond hit about **4.77%** and is inching toward 5% — the highest in a long time. Higher rates make expensive, fast-growing stocks (especially tech) less attractive.
- **The market now fears a rate hike, not a cut.** Because expensive oil makes inflation worse, traders now think there's about a **66% chance the Federal Reserve actually raises interest rates** at its September 16 meeting — up from 40% just a week ago. That is a big, unusual shift.
- **The jobs report Friday (Sept. 4) is the next big event.** This morning's smaller ADP jobs count came in weak (only 38,000 private jobs added). Weak jobs plus rising prices is the worst combo for stocks. Expect nervous, jumpy trading into Friday.

**Bottom line:** money is flowing *out* of tech and growth stocks and *into* oil, gold, defense, and nuclear/uranium. That theme shapes almost every pick below.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

**1. Exxon Mobil (XOM) — consider buying**
The biggest U.S. oil company. When oil prices jump like they are today, oil producers make more money, and their stocks usually rise. Exxon and its peers (Chevron, Occidental, ConocoPhillips) all climbed this week as crude spiked. This is the most direct, lower-drama way to bet that the oil price stays high or goes higher. **The catch:** if the U.S. and Iran suddenly calm down or announce a ceasefire, oil could drop fast and take these stocks down with it. This is a bet on the fighting *continuing*, so keep it small.

**2. American Airlines (AAL) — consider betting against it (expecting it to fall)**
Airlines are the opposite of oil companies: jet fuel is one of their biggest costs, so a spike in oil hurts them badly. American is the most fragile of the big airlines — it carries the most debt — so it tends to fall the hardest when fuel gets expensive. It already dropped several percent as oil rose. If oil keeps climbing, this could keep sliding. **The catch:** any sign of peace with Iran would send oil down and airlines sharply up, so this bet flips quickly.

**3. Lockheed Martin (LMT) — consider buying**
The world's largest defense (weapons and military) company. When there's active fighting involving the U.S. military, defense stocks tend to hold up or rise while the rest of the market falls, because investors expect more government military spending. It's a steadier "safety" pick for a scary, war-driven market. **The catch:** these stocks move slower than oil, so don't expect a fireworks move — it's more of a shelter than a rocket.

**4. SPDR Gold Shares (GLD) — consider buying**
This is a fund that simply tracks the price of gold. Gold is where nervous money hides when there's war, inflation, and stock-market fear all at once — exactly today's mix. Gold jumped about 14% just in August and is still strong. GLD is a calm, simple way to own that safe-haven trade without picking an individual miner. **The catch:** if the fear fades, gold can drift back down; it's protection, not a get-rich-quick trade.

**5. ProShares UltraShort Semiconductors (SOXS) — consider buying, but only a small amount and watch it closely**
This is a special fund that goes **up** when computer-chip stocks (like Nvidia and Micron) go **down** — and it moves about three times as fast as they do. Chip stocks are getting hit hardest right now because rising interest rates punish expensive tech. If chips keep falling this week, this fund rises quickly. **Big warning:** these "3x" funds are turbo-charged and are designed for very short holds (a day or a few days). They lose value if the market chops sideways, and they can wipe out gains fast if chips bounce. Treat this as a quick, small, high-risk trade — not something to hold for two weeks and forget.

## Also Watching (5 more)

**Cameco (CCJ) — consider buying.** A major uranium (nuclear fuel) miner. Uranium prices are climbing because miners keep cutting how much they'll produce while nuclear power demand (partly from AI data centers) keeps rising. It's a strong medium-term theme, and nuclear names are getting attention again this week.

**Occidental Petroleum (OXY) — consider buying.** Another oil producer, but it swings more than Exxon, so it can rise faster on an oil spike (and fall faster if oil reverses). A more aggressive way to play the same oil story as pick #1.

**ProShares UltraPro Short QQQ (SQQQ) — quick, small trade only.** Like SOXS, this fund goes **up** when the big tech-heavy market index falls, and it's turbo-charged (3x). With rate-hike fears hammering tech today, it can pop — but it's strictly a short-hold, high-risk tool, not a hold-for-two-weeks idea.

**Oklo (OKLO) — watch, don't chase.** A next-generation nuclear-reactor company that's a favorite of the AI-power theme. It just hit some real milestones and has analyst price targets well above today's price, but it's extremely jumpy — it can swing 8–9% in a single day. Interesting, but only for people comfortable with wild moves.

**GitLab (GTLB) — consider buying on strength.** One of the few tech names with good news: it reported strong quarterly results, record bookings, and raised its outlook, and rose about 8%. In a market punishing tech, a company beating expectations can stand out — but a falling overall market can still drag even good names down, so watch how it holds up.
