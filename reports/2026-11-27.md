## The big picture this morning

After a rough and scary November — the tech-heavy Nasdaq index had fallen about 14% from its high, which counts as a "correction" (a drop of 10% or more) — stocks are bouncing back hard this morning. The rebound is being led by the big artificial intelligence (AI) and computer-chip companies that had been beaten down the most. Nvidia was up more than 4% before the open, and other chip names like AMD, Micron, and Marvell were up 6% to 8%. The giant tech names (Google's parent Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, Oracle) were also up 2% to 5%.

In plain terms: the money that fled these stocks over the past few weeks is rushing back in today, at least for now. That makes this a day where the winners and losers are clearly split — the AI/chip crowd is up, while a few companies with their own bad news are down sharply.

Two other things in the background: oil prices are high (around $92 a barrel) because of worries about attacks on energy facilities in the Middle East, which helps oil and gas companies. And the crypto world (Bitcoin and related stocks) has been very weak lately, with Bitcoin near its lowest level in about three years, so be extra careful there.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

**1. Nvidia (NVDA) — up about 4% pre-market. Consider buying if the bounce holds.**
Nvidia is the most important stock in the whole AI story, and it's leading today's recovery. When beaten-down AI stocks come back, Nvidia usually leads the way. The simple idea: if this morning's rebound keeps going through the day, Nvidia is the cleanest way to ride it. The risk: this has been a very jumpy, back-and-forth market, and a bounce on a quiet half-day can fade just as fast as it came. Keep it small and don't chase it if it's already run up a lot by the time you look.

**2. Marvell Technology (MRVL) — up roughly 7–8% pre-market. Worth watching, with a known date coming up.**
Marvell makes custom AI chips for big cloud companies, and it's jumping today with the rest of the chip group. The extra reason to watch it: Marvell reports its latest quarterly results (its sales and profit numbers) on **Monday, December 1** — just a few days away, inside the 1-to-14-day window you'd be holding. Wall Street expects very strong growth (sales up more than 50% from a year ago). That report could send the stock sharply up or down. If you buy now hoping for a good report, understand you're taking a real gamble on that specific day — these reports are unpredictable. A more cautious move is to simply watch it and decide closer to the date.

**3. Vera Therapeutics (VERA) — jumped about 17% after a key announcement. Consider buying, carefully.**
This is a smaller drug company working on a kidney-disease treatment. Its stock shot up because U.S. health regulators (the FDA) just delayed a decision on a competing drug from a much bigger rival, Vertex. A delay for the rival gives Vera more breathing room and a potential head start. In plain words: good news for Vera's chances. The catch: small drug-company stocks are very volatile and move hard on single pieces of news, so only risk money you can afford to see swing around a lot.

**4. SOXL (3x Semiconductor ETF) — a turbo-charged way to bet on the chip rebound. Only for the brave.**
SOXL is a fund designed to move about three times as much as a basket of computer-chip stocks each day. If chips keep rising today, SOXL rises roughly three times as fast — but if chips fall, it drops three times as fast too. Because it's built to multiply one day's move, it is not meant to be held for long; the longer you hold it, the more its math can work against you. This is a short, aggressive way to play the chip bounce for a day or two at most — and an easy way to lose money quickly if the rebound reverses.

**5. Vertex Pharmaceuticals (VRTX) — down about 7%. Be cautious; this is a "wait and see," not an obvious buy.**
Vertex fell because the FDA pushed back its decision on an important new kidney drug (the new deadline is no later than February 28, 2027). That's a setback, but Vertex is a large, profitable company, so a 7% drop can sometimes attract bargain-hunters. Honestly, this one cuts both ways: it could keep sliding as disappointed owners sell, or it could steady and bounce. If you don't have a strong reason, the simplest choice is to just watch it for a day or two and see which way it settles before putting money in.
