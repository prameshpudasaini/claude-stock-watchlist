# November 25, 2026 — Short-Term Stock Watchlist

*Written early in the morning before the market opens (around 8:10 a.m. New York time). Today is a normal full trading day, but tomorrow (Thursday the 26th) is the Thanksgiving holiday and the market is closed, and Friday is a shortened half-day. So for anything you buy today, keep in mind that trading will be thin and jumpy for the rest of the week.*

## The big picture today (read this first)

This morning is shaping up to be a rough one, especially for technology stocks. Stock market futures (bets on where the market will open) are pointing sharply lower — the tech-heavy Nasdaq was down roughly 2% to 3% before the open. Several things are hitting at once:

- **Two important government reports come out at 8:30 a.m. New York time.** One measures inflation (called "Core PCE," the Federal Reserve's favorite inflation gauge) and one measures how fast the economy grew last quarter (GDP). Inflation is expected to come in a little hotter than last month. If it comes in even hotter than expected, stocks could fall more.
- **Here is the unusual part:** the Federal Reserve is now widely expected to *raise* interest rates at its December 8–9 meeting (markets put the odds near 97%). That is the opposite of the rate *cuts* people got used to. Higher interest rates are generally bad for expensive technology and growth stocks, and bad for Bitcoin.
- **Bitcoin is weak**, trading below $60,000 and down about 24% just this month. That is dragging down crypto-related stocks.
- **Nvidia is still a mess.** The company has been through a chaotic stretch of leadership drama (its longtime CEO resigned earlier this month, and the stock has swung wildly — up 31% on Monday, then down two days in a row). It was down about 5% again before the open today, and it is pulling the whole chip sector down with it.

Bottom line: the mood is nervous and leaning downward, driven by tech and chips. Be careful chasing anything today, and remember the holiday week makes prices extra choppy.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

**1. Ambarella (AMBA) — about $? (down roughly 35% to 40% before the open). Likely a "bet against it" / avoid situation.**
Ambarella makes computer-vision chips (the brains behind cameras in cars and security systems). It reported results last night and gave a weak forecast for its next quarter — its revenue outlook came in well below what Wall Street wanted. The stock is getting crushed, down around 35–40%. When a stock falls this hard on a bad forecast, it often keeps drifting lower for days as analysts cut their targets. This is not a stock to try to "catch" today. If anything, it is one to bet against if the selling keeps up — but a drop this big can also snap back violently, so if you are not experienced, the safest move is simply to stay away.

**2. Nvidia (NVDA) — down about 5% before the open. Watch closely; treat as high-risk.**
Nvidia is the most important stock in the market right now, and it is behaving wildly because of its leadership turmoil. It jumped 31% on Monday, then fell for two straight days. When a giant stock swings 5% to 30% in single days, that is a warning sign that nobody is sure what it is worth. For a short-term trade, this is a coin flip. The more careful play is to *wait* — let the dust settle rather than guessing which way the next big swing goes. If you do want exposure, keep the position small.

**3. A "bet against chips" fund — SOXS — rising as chip stocks fall. Consider buying for a very short hold only.**
SOXS is a fund designed to go *up* when semiconductor (computer chip) stocks go *down* — and it moves about three times as fast as the chip index, in either direction. With Nvidia, Micron, and others all falling this morning, this fund is likely rising. It is a way to profit from a down day in chips without betting against any single company. **Big warning:** these "3x" funds are only meant to be held for a day or two at most. They lose value over time if you hold them, and they can wipe out fast if chips suddenly bounce. Use a small amount and have an exit plan.

**4. Micron (MU) — down about 6% before the open. Likely more downside near-term; avoid or bet against.**
Micron makes memory chips. It is falling partly because a research firm (TrendForce) lowered its forecast for memory-chip prices, and partly because the whole chip group is weak today. With chip prices expected to soften and the sector under pressure, Micron looks more likely to keep sliding in the next few days than to bounce. Better to avoid buying here; aggressive traders might bet against it.

**5. Dell Technologies (DELL) — down about 6% before the open *despite* strong results. One to watch for a possible bounce.**
Here is an interesting one. Dell reported *good* numbers, including a record $132 billion backlog of orders for AI servers (money customers have committed to spend). Normally that is great news — but the stock fell anyway, dragged down by the general tech panic. When a company reports strong results but the stock drops only because the whole market is scared, it sometimes recovers once the panic fades. Keep an eye on Dell: if the overall market steadies in the next day or two, this could be a "buy the good company that got unfairly punished" opportunity. Do not rush in during a falling market, though — wait for signs it has stopped dropping.
