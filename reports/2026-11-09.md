## What's going on in the market today

Three big things are shaping today.

**First, the election aftermath.** Last week's U.S. midterm elections handed the Democrats control of both the Senate and the House. Stocks had a rough week right after the results came in — technology stocks especially got hit hard — but this morning (Monday) the market is steadying and trying to bounce back. One group is a clear standout winner: homebuilders. The thinking is that the new political setup makes it more likely that the taxes on imported building materials (called tariffs) get lowered. Those tariffs add thousands of dollars to the cost of building a house, so cheaper materials would be good news for homebuilders. That's why homebuilder stocks are jumping this morning.

**Second, a messy story at Nvidia.** Nvidia — the biggest name in AI computer chips — is falling about 4% to 5% this morning. This is *not* about its business, which is still strong. It's about its CEO, Jensen Huang. The company's board has opened an investigation after reports that Huang may have been the target of a foreign spying effort tied to a personal relationship, and Huang has publicly apologized. Lawmakers in Washington want him to testify in front of Congress on November 18. Because Nvidia is so big and so widely owned, when it drops it drags a lot of other chip and AI stocks down with it.

**Third, a big inflation report lands tomorrow.** On Tuesday morning the government releases its October inflation report (the official measure of how fast prices are rising). This is a market-mover: if prices rose faster than expected, it makes it less likely the Federal Reserve cuts interest rates soon, which tends to spook stocks; a cooler number tends to help stocks. So expect the whole market to get jumpy around that report tomorrow — something to keep in mind if you're buying today and planning to hold.

The clearest opportunities today are the homebuilder bounce, a couple of chip names, and some one-off news stories below.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

### 1. Homebuilders — consider buying
The single biggest fresh story this morning is the jump in homebuilding stocks after the election. **NVR (ticker: NVR)** is up more than **26%**, but one share of NVR costs several thousand dollars, which is out of reach for most people. A cheaper way to ride the same wave is a basket fund that holds all the big homebuilders at once — the **iShares U.S. Home Construction fund (ticker: ITB)** — or individual big builders like **D.R. Horton (DHI)** or **Lennar (LEN)**, whose shares cost far less than NVR. **This is a good group to consider buying** because the reason behind the move (hopes for lower material costs) could keep helping these stocks for several days. The risk: this is a bet on what politicians *might* do, not something that has actually happened yet, so if that optimism fades the stocks can give back their gains quickly.

### 2. AMD (AMD) — consider buying, but only if chips hold up
Pre-market **up about 3%**. AMD makes computer chips and is one of the main challengers to Nvidia in the AI race. It's rising this morning after a Wall Street firm (Oppenheimer) said nicer things about it and raised its rating. **Worth considering buying if the chip group stays steady today.** The big catch: the whole chip sector is nervous because of the Nvidia mess above. If Nvidia keeps sinking and drags the group down, AMD will likely fall with it. So before you buy, glance at how Nvidia and other chip names are trading — if they're sliding, wait.

### 3. Nvidia (NVDA) — be careful; avoid chasing it lower
Pre-market **down about 4% to 5%**. As explained up top, the drop is about a scandal involving the CEO, not about the company's actual sales, which are still booming. Here's the tricky part: because the business is fine, some bargain-hunters will try to buy the dip. But the cloud hanging over the stock — a congressional hearing coming up on November 18 and headlines that could get worse before they get better — can keep the price under pressure for days. **For now, lean toward staying patient rather than rushing in.** If you already own it, there's no business reason to panic-sell. If you're looking to bet against it for a quick trade, the scandal could keep it weak short-term — but that's a riskier game, because any "all clear" headline could snap it back up fast.

### 4. argenx (ARGX) — consider buying
Pre-market **up about 2%**. argenx is a drugmaker that's been one of the steadier winners in its field. This morning it announced it's buying a smaller company (Unnatural Products) that specializes in discovering new types of medicines, in a deal worth up to roughly $1.15 billion. Investors are treating it as a smart move. **A reasonable one to consider buying** if you want exposure to a strong, growing drug company rather than a jumpy meme-type stock. The move here is modest, so don't expect fireworks — this is a steadier idea, not a lottery ticket.

### 5. Zura Bio (ZURA) — stay away
Pre-market **down about 70%**. Zura Bio is a small drug developer, and this morning it announced that an important mid-stage trial of its skin-disease drug *failed* — the drug worked no better than a dummy treatment. The company is dropping that program entirely. **This is one to avoid completely.** Do not try to grab it as a "cheap bargain" after the crash — when a drug fails like this, the value tied to that drug is simply gone, and the stock can keep drifting lower. The only reason it's on this list is to warn you off it.
