# Short-Term Stock Watchlist — Wednesday, October 7, 2026

*A plain-English guide to today's best short-term trading ideas (hold roughly 1 to 14 days). This is research and opinion, not financial advice. Short-term trading is risky — never bet money you can't afford to lose.*

## What's driving the whole market today

The mood this Wednesday is cautious. The big U.S. stock indexes hit record highs yesterday (the tech-heavy Nasdaq set a fresh record), but this morning, before the opening bell, stock futures (bets on where the market opens) were pointing slightly **lower** — the S&P 500 fund was down about 0.3% and the Nasdaq-100 fund down about 0.65%. So after a strong run, the market is taking a small breather.

Here's the backdrop in plain words:

1. **The U.S. government is shut down — today is day 7.** Congress has been stuck since October 1 over a fight about health-care subsidies and spending, and betting markets now put the odds at about **70%** that it drags on past October 15. So far Wall Street is mostly ignoring it, but there's a catch: a shutdown delays official government reports (like the jobs and inflation numbers), which makes it harder for everyone to guess what the Federal Reserve will do next. The longer it lasts, the more that uncertainty builds.

2. **Interest rates in the bond market are still high.** The U.S. government's 10-year borrowing rate (the "10-year Treasury yield," a key number the whole market watches) sits around **5.31%**, and the 30-year is near **5.69%** — very high by the standards of recent years. High rates make borrowing pricier for companies and make safe bonds more tempting than stocks. Today there's a big 10-year government bond auction and the release of the Fed's last meeting notes (the "FOMC minutes"), both of which could nudge rates around.

3. **The Federal Reserve is expected to sit still.** After recent soft data, investors now see roughly a **78% chance** the Fed leaves interest rates unchanged at its next meeting. A Fed that holds steady is generally fine for stocks.

4. **Bitcoin just set a record.** Bitcoin climbed to an all-time high around **$126,000** yesterday, keeping every crypto-linked stock (Coinbase, Strategy, the Bitcoin miners) in the spotlight — though crypto swings hard in both directions.

The simple takeaway: the market is near record highs but pausing today, with high bond rates and a government shutdown hanging overhead. Don't chase anything blindly. Stick to names with their own fresh, clear reason to move, and keep every bet small.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

### 1. Marvell Technology (MRVL) — consider buying
Marvell makes specialized computer chips and the high-speed "optical" parts that move data around the giant data centers that run artificial intelligence. This morning an analyst upgraded the stock to a buy-style rating ("overweight") and **raised the price target to $150 from $105**, and the shares climbed about **5% to around $120**. **Consider buying** — Marvell is designing custom AI chips for huge customers (reportedly including Amazon and Microsoft), and AI chip stocks are one of the hottest, most in-demand corners of the market right now. A fresh upgrade from a respected analyst often pulls in more buyers over the following days. **The risk:** chip stocks are jumpy and have already run up a lot this year, so a bad day for the overall market — or any wobble in AI spending news — can knock this back quickly. Keep the position small and decide your exit price before you buy.

### 2. Applied Digital (APLD) — consider buying
Applied Digital builds and runs data centers — the big buildings full of computers — and rents them to companies doing artificial-intelligence work. The company just reported quarterly results that **crushed expectations**: about **$258 million in sales** when Wall Street expected roughly $100 million, and a small profit when a loss was expected. The stock was up about **4% before the bell**. **Consider buying** — this is a direct play on the enormous spending boom in AI infrastructure (the big cloud companies are expected to spend over $600 billion this year), and a blowout earnings report can keep lifting a stock for several days. **The risk:** this is a smaller, faster-moving company that has swung hard in the past, and it has had some insider selling and rising costs. It can give back a post-earnings pop fast if the whole market turns down.

### 3. Caribou Biosciences (CRBU) — consider betting against (or simply avoid)
Caribou is a small gene-editing drug company. It just delivered terrible news: it is **giving up on both of its main experimental cancer treatments**, looking into selling the company or other "strategic alternatives," and cutting a large chunk of its staff. The stock was crashing about **39%** before the bell. **This could be a good one to bet against if it keeps falling** (for experienced traders who know how to do that safely), or simply a name to **avoid buying** even though it now looks cheap. **The risk:** a stock that has already collapsed can bounce sharply on any rumor of a buyer stepping in, so betting against it is not a sure thing. The company still has over $100 million in cash, which means a bargain-hunting acquirer could appear and spike the price.

### 4. NetApp (NTAP) — consider buying
NetApp is a company that sells data-storage systems — the equipment businesses use to keep and manage all their digital information, increasingly for AI projects. This morning the research firm Evercore upgraded the stock and **lifted its price target sharply to $300 from $210**, pointing to faster sales growth next year. The shares were up a little over **1.5%** before the bell. **Consider buying** — a big price-target jump from a major firm often draws steady buying, and NetApp rides the same AI-data wave as the flashier chip names but with a calmer, more established business. **The risk:** this is a slower-moving, larger company, so don't expect a huge pop — gains here are likely to be gradual, and a modest 1.5% premarket move shows the market reaction is measured, not explosive.

### 5. Constellation Energy (CEG) — consider buying (with caution)
Constellation is the largest nuclear-power company in the United States. Yesterday it announced a **20-year deal to sell electricity to Google**, which needs vast, round-the-clock power for its AI computers, and the stock jumped about **12%**. **Consider buying** — this fits the single hottest theme in the market (powering AI), it's a real, long-lasting, big-money contract, and these multi-day news stories sometimes keep drawing buyers. **The risk — and it's a real one:** the stock already made its big jump yesterday, so the easy gain is gone and you'd be buying after the pop. On top of that, steady-dividend companies like utilities get less attractive when bond interest rates are high, and rates are high right now. This is the riskiest of today's five on timing — only consider a small position.
