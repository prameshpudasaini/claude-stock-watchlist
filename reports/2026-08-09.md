## Backdrop (why the tape looks the way it does)

Friday was a **"bad news is good news"** session. July payrolls came in at **-23,000 jobs** (an outright loss vs. +83k expected), unemployment steady at 4.1%. With 2026 inflation still hot from the oil-price shock, the Fed had been leaning hawkish — so a weak labor print flipped the narrative from "risk of a hike" to "Fed on hold, cuts back on the table." Traders bought risk hard:

- **S&P 500 +0.62% to a record 7,757.64**
- **Nasdaq +1.3% to 26,690.62**
- **Dow +0.28% to 54,036.93** — strongest week since April
- **Bitcoin back above $65,000**, ETH ~$1,925, gold at records — everything rate-sensitive caught a bid.

Fed-funds futures now price roughly a **90% chance of a September cut**. But this is the tension for the week: labor is weakening while inflation is sticky. **The referee is Wednesday's CPI (Aug 12, 8:30am ET, covers July).** A hot number reintroduces the "Fed can't cut" fear right into a market at record highs; an in-line/soft number keeps the melt-up alive. A lot of good news is already priced — chase carefully with tight stops.
