# Short-Term Stock Watchlist — September 9, 2026

*For someone who wants to buy today and hold anywhere from 1 to 14 days. Written in plain English. This is research and education, not financial advice. Short-term trading is risky, and small, thinly-traded stocks are the riskiest of all — they can fall as fast as they rose.*

---

## The mood this morning (read this first)

Three things are pushing the market around today:

1. **Oil keeps climbing.** Overnight the U.S. military said it destroyed five Iranian oil tankers after Iran tried to attack U.S. warships. That has pushed Brent crude (the main global oil price) above $100 a barrel — the highest since July — and U.S. oil has now risen six days in a row. Expensive oil is good for energy and defense companies and bad for almost everyone else, because it raises costs and stokes inflation worry.

2. **Interest rates could actually go UP.** This is the big, unusual worry. Because oil is making inflation worse, bond traders now see a roughly 59% chance the Federal Reserve (the U.S. central bank) *raises* interest rates at its meeting next week (September 15–16), instead of cutting. The 10-year Treasury yield — a key measure of long-term rates — is near 4.82%, its highest since 2023. Higher rates hurt expensive, fast-growing tech stocks and risky bets like crypto the most.

3. **Apple's big product event is today.** Apple unveils new iPhones and other devices this afternoon — its first major launch under its new CEO. This tends to move Apple and its suppliers.

The result: technology-heavy Nasdaq futures are lower, and money is again flowing **into** oil and defense and **out of** expensive tech and crypto. There's also an inflation report coming later this week that everyone is watching. Keep your position sizes small today.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

### 1. ExxonMobil (XOM) — Oil & gas — *Consider buying*
Exxon is the simplest way to ride today's oil spike. When crude jumps the way it has this week, the biggest oil producers usually make more money and their stocks tend to climb with it. Energy was one of the only groups rising before the opening bell (the energy sector fund XLE was up about 1%, and Exxon up about 1%).
- **Why it matters today:** Oil above $100 directly boosts Exxon's profits, and the news flow (U.S.–Iran conflict) is fresh.
- **The catch:** You are really betting the oil price stays high for the next week or two. If tensions suddenly cool — a ceasefire, or the fighting eases — oil can drop fast and pull the stock down with it.

### 2. Signet Jewelers (SIG) — Jewelry retail — *Consider buying if it holds its gains*
Signet (which owns Kay, Zales, and Jared) jumped about 9%–12% before the open after strong quarterly results. It earned an adjusted $2.19 per share versus the roughly $1.72 Wall Street expected, and — more importantly — it *raised* its profit forecast for the full year. When a company both beats and lifts its outlook, the good mood can carry the stock for several days.
- **Why it matters today:** This is a real earnings beat plus a raised forecast, not hype.
- **The catch:** A big jump can fade if the whole market sells off on the oil-and-rates worry. It's usually safer to see it hold its early gains than to chase the very first pop.

### 3. Thermo Fisher Scientific (TMO) — Healthcare & lab equipment — *Consider buying*
The bank UBS upgraded Thermo Fisher to "Buy" this morning and lifted its price target sharply to $730 from $540 — a large vote of confidence. Thermo Fisher makes lab and medical equipment, a steadier, less flashy business that can hold up better than tech when interest rates are rising.
- **Why it matters today:** A fresh, strong analyst upgrade is a clear reason for new buyers to step in, and this is a calmer pick for a jittery day.
- **The catch:** Lower-drama also means smaller moves — don't expect a fireworks jump. And a single upgrade can be shrugged off if the overall market has a rough day.

### 4. Apple (AAPL) — Consumer electronics — *Watch closely; be cautious about buying ahead of the event*
Apple reveals new iPhones and other products this afternoon, its first big launch under the new CEO. Events like this get a lot of attention, and Apple's suppliers often move too.
- **Why it matters today:** A genuine, scheduled catalyst that can swing the stock either way within hours.
- **The catch (important):** Apple stock very often *falls* right after these launches, even when the products are good — traders buy the rumor ahead of time and then sell once the news is out. With rising interest rates already weighing on big tech, the safer move is usually to watch how it reacts rather than guess ahead of the announcement.

### 5. ServiceTitan (TTAN) — Business software — *Consider betting against it (expecting it to fall) if weakness continues*
ServiceTitan, which makes software for home-service businesses, dropped about 17% before the open. Its results were actually fine, but its forecast for the next few months came in light, and one Wall Street firm (Canaccord) cut its price target to $90 from $105. Soft guidance from a pricey software stock tends to keep pressure on the shares for a while.
- **Why it matters today:** A weak outlook is a real, lasting negative, and expensive software is exactly what suffers most when interest rates are rising.
- **The catch:** The stock has already fallen a lot, so some of the bad news is priced in — a bounce is possible. Betting against a stock (profiting if it falls) carries its own risk: if it rises instead, losses can grow. Keep it small.

## Big Movers

*Honest note: in my scan this morning I did not find a U.S.-listed stock clearly up or down about 40% or more today on news. The biggest clean, news-driven moves were in the roughly 10%–26% range. Here they are, with warnings.*

- **SUNation Energy (SUNE), up ~26% (to around $2.98):** This tiny solar company's merger partner, Suniva, completed an $835 million fundraising to build a big U.S. solar-cell factory and roughly quadruple capacity. **Risk: this is a very small, thinly-traded stock trading under $3. Moves like this can reverse violently, and the deal still has to close later this year. Treat it as a gamble, not an investment.**
- **ServiceTitan (TTAN), down ~17%:** Weak forward forecast overshadowed a decent quarter (see Top 5 above). Risk: the drop is large and a short-lived bounce is possible even if the longer trend stays down.
- **Signet Jewelers (SIG), up ~9%–12%:** Strong earnings and a raised full-year outlook (see Top 5 above). Risk: much of the good news is now in the price after the pop.
- **Casey's General Stores (CASY), down ~8%–12%:** The convenience-store and gas chain beat on profit but reported soft same-store sales and left its full-year outlook unchanged, which disappointed. Risk: don't assume it snaps right back — a stalled sales outlook can weigh on it for a while.
- **Amgen (AMGN), still weak after a ~10% drop yesterday:** The drugmaker had its worst day since 2000 after an experimental cholesterol drug (pelacarsen, developed with partners) failed a major heart-disease trial. Risk: a failed trial is a real, lasting setback, not a quick dip — be careful assuming a fast rebound.

*If any tiny stock rockets 40% or more on a single headline today, treat it with extreme caution: those moves often reverse just as fast, and thin trading means you can get stuck.*

- Apple's event this afternoon can swing that stock quickly — watching the reaction is usually safer than guessing ahead of it.
