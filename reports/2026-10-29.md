## The Big Picture Today (read this first)

The mood this morning is nervous, and it's mostly about one company: **Google.**

1. **Google's parent company, Alphabet (GOOGL), is down about 10% before the market opened.** Its quarterly results actually beat expectations on overall sales and profit — but its cloud-computing business (renting out computing power and storage to other companies) grew slower than Wall Street wanted, and the company is spending enormous amounts of money building out artificial-intelligence (AI) systems. Investors got spooked that all that spending isn't paying off fast enough. Because Google is one of the biggest companies in the world, its drop is pulling the whole tech-heavy Nasdaq down with it (Nasdaq futures were down about 1.4% before the open).

2. **Microsoft (MSFT) went the other way — up about 2.6%** — after its results beat expectations. So today you have a split screen: one AI giant rewarded, one punished. That tells you investors are getting pickier about which AI companies are actually delivering.

3. **The Federal Reserve left interest rates unchanged yesterday** (at a range of 3.75%–4.00%). The Fed is the group that sets the country's main interest rate. The vote was 10-to-2, with two officials actually wanting to *raise* rates. The new Fed chair, Kevin Warsh, refused to promise a rate cut or hike in December, which kept everyone guessing. Longer-term borrowing costs ticked up (the 10-year government bond yield rose to about 5.05%), which is a mild headwind for stocks.

4. **The next big event is tonight: Amazon (AMZN) reports after the market closes** (around 5:00 PM Eastern). Like Google, the key number everyone will stare at is how fast its cloud business (called AWS) is growing and how much it's spending on AI. Traders are betting on a swing of roughly **7%** either way. After Google's stumble, the bar is nervous — a strong AWS number could calm the whole market; a weak one could make the "AI spending is out of control" worry even louder.

**What this means for you:** This is a choppy, headline-driven day. The safest approach is to let the dust settle — especially to wait and see how Amazon's results land tonight before making big tech bets. If you do trade today, keep your bets small.
