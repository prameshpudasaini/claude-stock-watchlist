## The Big Picture Today (read this first)

The whole market is in a **"show me the money" mood about artificial intelligence (AI)**, and it's ugly for tech this morning.

Here's what happened. Over the last two days, four of the biggest companies in the world reported their quarterly results, and investors didn't like what they heard:

- **Google/Alphabet** reported earlier this week and its stock fell hard.
- **Amazon** and **Intel** reported after the market closed yesterday (Thursday), and both are falling sharply today.

The common worry is simple: these companies are spending *enormous* amounts of money building AI — data centers, chips, power — and investors are starting to ask, "When does all this spending actually turn into bigger profits?" Amazon alone said its AI spending (already about $220 billion this year) will go "meaningfully" higher in 2027. Wall Street got scared that profits are being eaten up by the build-out.

**The result:** Technology-stock futures (early bets before the market opens) were down roughly **2% to 2.5%** this morning. The group of computer-chip stocks is now about **21% below** its high from June. Money is flowing *out* of tech and chips and *into* safer, boring names like food and household-goods companies.

Bitcoin is also weak, trading around **$77,600**, down from about $80,000 earlier.

**What this means for you:** Today is a "risk-off" day — meaning people are scared and selling the exciting stuff. Catching a falling tech stock today is dangerous; it can keep dropping. The safer short-term ideas today are either (a) the defensive names people are buying *into*, or (b) simply waiting for the panic to calm down before buying the quality tech names at lower prices. Trade small.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

### 1. Amazon (AMZN) — wait for it to stop falling, then consider buying
- **Move:** Down about **7%** before the open (to roughly $227).
- **Why:** Amazon's results actually had a huge bright spot — its cloud-computing business (AWS, which rents computing power to other companies) grew **39%**, its fastest in nearly five years, and it has a giant $578 billion pile of future contracts lined up. But investors punished the stock because profits slightly missed and the company is spending so heavily on AI. Goldman Sachs still rates it a "buy."
- **Plain-English take:** The business underneath is strong — this looks more like fear than a broken company. But **do not try to catch it while it's still dropping today.** The smart move is to **watch for the fall to stop** (a day or two where it stops making new lows), then consider buying for a bounce. **Risk:** if the whole market keeps sinking on AI-spending fears, it can fall further first.

### 2. Intel (INTC) — stay away, or a careful bet-against
- **Move:** Down about **25%** before the open — a massive one-day drop.
- **Why:** Intel actually beat on last quarter's profit, but its forecast for the next few months was weak. It expects its personal-computer chip business to shrink by more than 20%, it's dealing with a shortage of memory chips that's raising its costs, and it faces a year of burning through cash. Its own revenue forecast ($15–16 billion) came in well below what Wall Street wanted ($17 billion).
- **Plain-English take:** These are **real, lasting problems**, not a one-day scare. Don't try to be a hero and "buy the dip" here. If anything, this could keep sliding, so it's a **possible one to bet against** (betting it falls more) — but betting against a stock that already crashed 25% is risky because any small bounce hurts fast. For most people: **just stay away.**

### 3. SOXS (inverse chip-stock fund) — a short-term bet that chips keep falling
- **What it is:** SOXS is a fund that goes **up** when computer-chip stocks go **down** (it's built to move about 3× the opposite of the chip group each day).
- **Why today:** Chip stocks are getting hammered — Intel down 25%, AMD down about 10%, Dell down about 13%. If this fear continues for another day or two, a fund like SOXS rises.
- **Plain-English take:** This is a way to **make money if the tech selloff keeps going**, without betting against one single company. **Big warning:** these "3× daily" funds lose value over time if the market chops sideways, so they are **for a very short hold only** (a day or two), not something to buy and forget. Only for people comfortable with fast, risky trades.

### 4. Mondelez (MDLZ) — consider buying as a "safe harbor"
- **Move:** Up about **7%** before the open.
- **Why:** Mondelez makes snacks (Oreo, Cadbury, Ritz). It reported higher sales and **kept its full-year forecast steady**. On a day when investors are fleeing risky tech, boring, steady companies like this one attract money.
- **Plain-English take:** This is a classic **"hide here when things are scary"** stock. If the tech fear drags on for the next week or two, money keeps rotating into steady names like food companies. A **calmer buy candidate** than anything in tech today. **Risk:** if tech suddenly bounces back, these safe names can give back some of today's gains.

### 5. Nvidia (NVDA) — the quality name to watch for a bounce
- **Move:** Down only about **3%** — holding up far better than the rest of the chip group.
- **Why:** Nvidia makes the chips that power AI. Unlike Intel, it barely sells PC chips, so Intel's problems don't really hit it. It's falling only because the whole group is falling, not because of its own news.
- **Plain-English take:** When the dust settles, the strongest company usually recovers first. **Watch it today, don't rush.** If the selling stops and it starts climbing again, it's the **best-quality name to buy** for a bounce over the next week or two. **Risk:** it reports its own results in a few weeks, and the market mood is fragile right now.
