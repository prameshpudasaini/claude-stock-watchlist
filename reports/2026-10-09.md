## What's driving the whole market today

After a rough Thursday, the mood this Friday morning is calmer and a little more hopeful. Before the opening bell, U.S. stock futures (bets on where the market opens) were pointing **slightly up** — the S&P 500 fund up about 0.3%, the Nasdaq-100 fund up about 0.7%, and the Dow up a touch. Two things that scared people yesterday are easing off this morning: **oil prices came back down**, and **technology stocks are bouncing back**.

Here's the backdrop in simple terms:

1. **Yesterday was ugly for tech, today is a rebound.** On Thursday the tech-heavy Nasdaq-100 dropped about 1.4% — its worst single day in seven weeks — and a basket of computer-chip stocks fell about 3.4%. The trigger was fresh worry about whether the huge amounts of money being poured into artificial intelligence (AI) will actually pay off, set off by questions about how much revenue OpenAI (the company behind ChatGPT) is really bringing in. This morning those same AI and chip names are recovering, which is lifting the whole market.

2. **Oil backing off is a relief.** Earlier in the week oil spiked on Middle East tension; today it's falling again. Cheaper oil takes some pressure off inflation (prices rising) worries and helps the companies that get hurt by expensive fuel.

3. **Interest rates are still the heavy weight in the background.** The Federal Reserve (the U.S. central bank that sets interest rates) raised rates in September for the first time in three years and has hinted more could come. Inflation expectations have crept up (people now expect about 3.9% over the next year, the highest in a while). High rates make safe bonds more tempting than stocks and hit expensive, fast-growing tech hardest — so any tech rebound can stall quickly.

4. **Earnings season is kicking off.** The big quarterly-report season is starting. The airline Delta reported this morning — and the reaction was poor (more below).

The two biggest *fresh* stories today, though, aren't about the overall market — they're two pieces of government/industry news that are shaking up whole groups of stocks: **new Medicare quality scores** (lifting some health-insurance companies and sinking others) and a **wireless-airwaves (spectrum) deal involving Elon Musk's SpaceX** (hurting the big phone carriers but helping the companies that own cell towers). That's where today's clearest opportunities are. Keep every bet small.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

### 1. Humana (HUM) — up about 14%–16% before the bell — consider buying
Humana is one of the largest sellers of **Medicare Advantage** plans (private health-insurance plans for older Americans, paid for largely by the government). Overnight, the government (CMS) released its new **2027 "Star Ratings"** — quality scores that decide how much bonus money each insurer gets. Humana's most important plan got a better score, and the stock jumped. **Consider buying** — better star ratings translate fairly directly into more government money next year, so this is real, concrete good news, not hype, and the group tends to keep drawing buyers for a few days after a rating surprise. **The risk:** a big one-morning jump like this can partly fade as the day goes on, and these insurance stocks have been very jumpy all year on Medicare headlines. Don't chase it if it runs up much more before you get in.

### 2. Clover Health (CLOV) — up about 11% before the bell — consider buying (higher risk)
Clover Health is a smaller Medicare Advantage insurer. It rose because its main plans earned a **top 5-star rating for 2027** — the best score the government gives. **Consider buying** — a 5-star rating is a genuine badge of quality that brings in more members and more bonus money, and good news on a small company like this can keep pushing the stock for several days. **The risk:** Clover is a smaller, more volatile stock than Humana, so it swings harder in both directions, and some of today's pop may simply unwind. Keep the position small.

### 3. Crown Castle (CCI) and American Tower (AMT) — up about 8% and 6% before the bell — consider buying
These two companies own the physical cell towers and rooftop antennas that wireless carriers rent to run their networks. They jumped this morning on news tied to a **spectrum (wireless-airwaves) deal involving SpaceX** — investors are betting that more players fighting over airwaves means more demand to build out and rent network equipment, which is good for the companies that own the towers. **Consider buying** — these are steadier, income-paying businesses (not speculative tech), so a jump backed by a real industry shift can hold up better than a hype spike. **The risk:** the spectrum story is still partly rumor (Elon Musk has publicly played it down in the past), so if it fizzles, today's pop could give some back. These stocks also fall when interest rates rise, which is a live worry right now.

### 4. Lumentum (LITE) — up about 6% before the bell — consider buying (higher risk)
Lumentum makes the lasers and optical parts that shuttle data between the computers inside AI data centers. Its stock rose as part of today's rebound in AI-related names, helped by the company's message that its products are **sold out and booked solid well into 2027** because demand is so strong. **Consider buying** — this is one of the clearest "picks-and-shovels" ways to ride the AI build-out (it sells gear every AI company needs), and the stock has been a huge winner this year. **The risk — and it's real:** this is exactly the kind of expensive AI stock that got hammered yesterday on bubble worries. If the AI-spending fear comes back, it can drop fast. Today is a bounce; treat it as a short, nimble trade, not a set-and-forget.

### 5. Delta Air Lines (DAL) — down about 4% before the bell — avoid buying / possible bet-against
Delta reported its quarterly results this morning, and the market didn't like them — the outlook came in **weaker than Wall Street hoped**, and the stock fell. **This is one to avoid buying, or even to bet against** if the weakness continues — a disappointing report plus a soft forecast tends to weigh on an airline stock for several days, and a weak Delta often drags the other airlines down with it. **The risk with betting against it:** the stock already dropped before the market even opened, so a lot of the bad news may be priced in, and a bounce is always possible after a sharp fall. It's usually safer to watch how it trades for a bit rather than jump in right at the open.
