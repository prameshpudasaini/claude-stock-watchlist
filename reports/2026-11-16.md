## The big picture today (read this first)

Two things are driving the whole market this week, and they pull in opposite directions:

1. **The Federal Reserve now looks likely to RAISE interest rates in December, not cut them.** This is a big shift. A strong October jobs report (about 165,000 new jobs, unemployment 4.1%) plus sticky inflation has flipped the mood. Betting markets now put the odds of a December rate increase at roughly 88% to 100%. Higher interest rates are bad for expensive, fast-growing tech stocks (they make future profits worth less today), so money has been leaving those names. The notes from the Fed's last meeting come out **Wednesday** and could add fuel either way.

2. **Nvidia reports its earnings Tuesday after the market closes.** This is the single most important event of the week. Nvidia is the biggest company tied to the artificial-intelligence (AI) boom, and the whole chip and AI group tends to move with it. Traders expect a swing of about 8% to 10% in Nvidia's stock the day after — huge for a company that size. AI stocks have already dropped hard over the past week on worries that companies are spending too much on AI, so nerves are high.

On top of that, big retailers report this week: **Home Depot (Tuesday), Target (Wednesday), and Walmart (Thursday).** And the blockbuster video game **Grand Theft Auto 6 launches Wednesday, November 19.**

Bottom line: this is a jumpy, "play-it-safe" kind of market where higher interest rates are punishing expensive tech. Be careful, keep positions small, and don't bet the house ahead of Nvidia's report.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

### 1. Nvidia (NVDA) — the week's main event, but a coin flip
Nvidia was down about 3% to 4% in early trading and has fallen roughly 12% over the past week. It reports its quarterly results **Tuesday after the close.** This is the stock everyone is watching. **Honest take: this is a gamble, not a sure thing.** Traders are betting on an 8%-to-10% move the day after, which can go either way. If the results are great and the company sounds confident, it could bounce hard and drag the whole market up with it. If there's any disappointment, it could fall sharply in a market that's already scared about AI overspending. **What to do:** If you're new to this, the safer move is to watch and wait rather than bet right before the report. If you do take a side, keep it small — this is as close to a 50/50 flip as it gets.

### 2. A "bet-against-tech" fund like SOXS or SQQQ — a way to profit if the slide continues
These are special funds that go **up** when technology stocks go **down** (SOXS rises when computer-chip stocks fall; SQQQ rises when the big-tech Nasdaq index falls). With the Fed leaning toward raising rates and AI stocks already sinking, this is a direct way to bet that the pain keeps going for a few more days. **What to do:** Consider buying a small amount if you think tech keeps falling into and after Nvidia's report. **Big risk:** these funds are turbo-charged and lose value fast if tech bounces — and a strong Nvidia report Tuesday could spark exactly that bounce. Treat this as a short, nimble bet, not something to hold for two weeks and forget.

### 3. Take-Two Interactive (TTWO) — riding the Grand Theft Auto 6 launch
Take-Two makes the Grand Theft Auto games, and **GTA 6 — one of the most anticipated games ever — launches Wednesday, November 19.** The stock has been climbing toward record highs on the excitement, and the company has stuck to its sales forecast. This is a nice feature of this pick: it's driven by its own story (a giant game launch), so it can hold up even while AI and tech stocks wobble. **What to do:** Consider buying to ride the launch buzz. **The catch:** when a long-awaited event finally happens, the stock sometimes falls anyway because traders "sell the news" after waiting so long. If you buy, have a plan to take profits around the launch rather than getting greedy.

### 4. Walmart (WMT) — a safer place to hide, reports Thursday
When people get scared about the economy and higher rates, they often move money into steady, everyday-essentials companies. Walmart is the classic example — people shop there no matter what. It reports earnings **Thursday.** **What to do:** Consider buying as a calmer, lower-drama way to stay invested while tech is shaky. **The catch:** it still has an earnings report Thursday, so there's some risk of a surprise move. This is a "sleep-at-night" pick, not a get-rich-quick one.

### 5. Home Depot (HD) — reports Tuesday, and higher rates work against it — lean cautious
Home Depot reports **Tuesday morning.** Here's the problem: when interest rates go up, mortgages and home-improvement loans get more expensive, so people do fewer big remodeling projects — and that hurts Home Depot. Going into its report in a rising-rate week, the setup looks risky. **What to do:** This is one to be careful with, or even to bet against if it keeps dropping after the report. If you already own it, don't be surprised by a rough reaction. Don't chase it higher just because it's a famous name.
