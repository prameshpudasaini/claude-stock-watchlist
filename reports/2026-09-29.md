# Short-Term Stock Watchlist — Tuesday, September 29, 2026

*A plain-English guide to today's best short-term trading ideas (hold roughly 1 to 14 days). This is research and opinion, not financial advice. Short-term trading is risky — never bet money you can't afford to lose.*

## What's driving the whole market today

Stocks are starting the day roughly flat, with a slight lean upward before the opening bell (the main indexes were up between about 0.2% and 0.3%). Computer-chip stocks are showing some fresh strength this morning. But underneath that calm surface, there is one really big worry you need to understand:

**Interest rates are going the "wrong" way.** Normally people hope the Federal Reserve (the U.S. central bank that sets interest rates) will *cut* rates, which usually helps stocks. Right now the opposite is happening. The interest rate on 10-year U.S. government bonds jumped to about 5.27% — the highest in 19 years — and traders now think there is about a 70% chance the Fed *raises* rates again in October (a month ago that chance was under 18%). Higher rates make borrowing more expensive and make expensive stocks (like fast-growing tech) less attractive.

Two other things matter today:
1. **Oil is still high** because of tension between the U.S. and Iran (a peace deal to reopen a key oil shipping lane fell through). High oil helps oil and defense companies and adds to inflation worries.
2. **It's a jobs week.** Several employment reports come out, ending with the big monthly jobs report on Friday (economists expect a weak-ish number, around 85,000 new jobs). A weak jobs number plus rate-hike fears is an uncomfortable mix, so expect nervous, choppy trading.

The simple takeaway: the market looks calm this morning, but rising interest rates are the storm cloud overhead. Be careful with expensive, high-flying stocks, and pay attention to companies that have their *own* piece of news today — those are the cleaner bets.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

### 1. Summit Therapeutics (SMMT) — consider buying
This is the standout news story of the day. AstraZeneca, one of the world's biggest drug companies, agreed to invest **$2 billion** into Summit and to work together on Summit's lung-cancer drug (called ivonescimab). AstraZeneca is paying $18.36 per share, which was about a 19% premium to Friday's price — in plain words, a giant, respected company is voluntarily paying *extra* to own a piece of Summit. On top of that, Summit's late-stage trial data showed its drug helped lung-cancer patients live longer than the current standard treatments. The stock jumped about **17% to 23%** before the market opened. **Consider buying** — a $2 billion vote of confidence from a major partner is a strong, real reason for the stock to stay elevated. **The risk:** the stock already leapt a lot in one morning, so it could give back some of that jump; keep any position modest and don't chase it if it spikes much higher after the open.

### 2. Fair Isaac (FICO) — a good one to bet against
Fair Isaac makes the FICO credit score — the number that has decided who gets a mortgage in America for decades, giving it a near-monopoly. Today that monopoly is being cracked open: the government housing regulator is letting mortgage lenders use a cheaper, competing credit score (called VantageScore, which costs under $1 versus FICO's $10-plus). Mortgages are about **62% of FICO's scoring revenue**, so this is a direct hit to its most profitable business. The stock fell roughly **15% to 17%**. **This could be a good one to bet against (bet that it keeps falling)** as investors digest the loss of its pricing power. **The risk:** it has already dropped a lot and beaten-down stocks can bounce sharply for a day or two, so don't assume the fall is a straight line down.

### 3. Nvidia (NVDA) — consider buying
Nvidia, the most important AI-chip company, just announced it will buy back another **$150 billion** of its own stock — the biggest buyback increase in U.S. history, bringing the total to $235 billion. When a company buys back its own shares, it signals management thinks the stock is cheap and it puts steady buying pressure underneath the price. Nvidia rose about 1.7% and is helping chip stocks recover this morning. **Consider buying** if chip stocks keep firming up today. **The risk:** rising interest rates are hardest on expensive tech stocks, and a rival memory-chip maker (Micron) reports its results tonight (see pick #5) — a bad report there could drag Nvidia down with it.

### 4. Netflix (NFLX) — consider buying
Netflix had a rough September, with its stock under steady selling pressure. This morning, Deutsche Bank (a big bank) upgraded it to "Buy," essentially saying the drop went too far and it's time to own it again. An upgrade from a major bank often brings in fresh buyers over the following days. **Consider buying** on this turn in sentiment. **The risk:** if the whole market sells off because of the interest-rate fears above, even good news on one stock can get drowned out for a day.

### 5. Micron Technology (MU) — a high-risk trade around tonight's earnings
Micron makes memory chips (the parts that store data), which are in huge demand for AI. It reports its quarterly results **after the market closes today**, and it's seen as a health check for the whole AI and chip world. Expectations are very high (analysts expect around $50 billion in sales). This is a coin-flip event: a strong report could send Micron *and* other chip stocks up tomorrow, while a disappointment could sink them. **Only for people comfortable with a gamble** — if you buy before the report you're betting on the outcome, and a miss can cause a fast drop. Many careful traders prefer to wait until *after* the report and buy only if the results are clearly good.

The market looks quiet this morning, but the real story is rising interest rates and a Federal Reserve that might raise rates instead of cutting — a backdrop that can turn calm days into sharp sell-offs, especially for expensive tech and risky bets like crypto.
