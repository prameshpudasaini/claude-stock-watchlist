# Short-Term Stock Watchlist — September 11, 2026

*For someone who wants to buy today and hold anywhere from 1 to 14 days. Written in plain English. This is research and education, not financial advice. Short-term trading is risky, and small, thinly-traded stocks are the riskiest of all — they can fall as fast as they rose.*

---

## The mood this morning (read this first)

The big inflation report everyone was waiting for came out this morning at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, and it ran a little hot. Here is what that means in plain words:

1. **Prices are still rising faster than hoped.** The Consumer Price Index (CPI) — the main monthly measure of how fast the cost of everyday things is going up — showed prices were 3.7% higher than a year ago. Experts had guessed 3.6%. Month-to-month, prices rose 0.4% when 0.3% was expected. It's only a small miss, but it points the wrong way.

2. **Now people are betting the Federal Reserve might RAISE interest rates next week.** The Federal Reserve (the U.S. central bank) meets September 15–16 to decide on rates. Because inflation stayed sticky, traders now put the odds of a rate *increase* somewhere around 55–70%. Higher interest rates make borrowing more expensive and tend to hurt the most expensive, fast-growing tech stocks and anything tied to Bitcoin. This is the single biggest thing hanging over the market today.

3. **Oil is expensive — above $100 a barrel.** Tension between the U.S. and Iran has pushed crude oil over $100. That's good for oil and defense companies but bad for almost everyone else, because it keeps inflation worry alive.

4. **Bitcoin dropped.** Bitcoin fell below $77,000 (around $76,500), down about 2%, as the rate-hike fear scared off buyers. Crypto-linked stocks usually feel this even harder.

Despite the hot inflation number, the overall market opened fairly calm — early trading pointed slightly higher, with tech and small companies holding up. The takeaway: it's a nervous, headline-driven day heading into next week's Fed decision. Keep your bets small, and don't chase anything that has already jumped a lot.

## Top 5 Picks for Today

### 1. Oracle (ORCL) — Cloud & AI software — *Consider buying, but it has already jumped*
Oracle is the clear winner of the morning. It reported its quarterly results last night, and the numbers were excellent: sales grew 30% from a year ago, and — the part investors care about most — its backlog of future cloud orders (business it has signed but not yet delivered) ballooned to about $664 billion, far above what Wall Street expected. It signed more than $30 billion of new artificial-intelligence cloud contracts in just three months. The stock rose about 5–6% before the opening bell (to roughly $161).
- **Why it matters today:** This is the cleanest piece of good news out there, and it's specific to Oracle, so it can keep going up even if the wider market is jittery about interest rates.
- **The catch:** It already gapped up several percent, so a chunk of the good news is now in the price. Buying after a big jump means you're paying up. If you want in, consider a smaller amount and don't chase it if it runs away from you.

### 2. ExxonMobil (XOM) — Oil & gas — *Consider buying on the oil theme*
With crude oil sitting above $100 a barrel because of U.S.–Iran tension, big oil companies make more money on every barrel they sell. On a day when high inflation is scaring people away from expensive tech, steady energy companies are one of the few groups that actually *benefit* from the thing everyone is worried about.
- **Why it matters today:** Expensive oil directly helps Exxon's profits, and energy stocks often attract steady buying when oil is this high.
- **The catch:** This trade lives and dies on the oil price. If the U.S. and Iran suddenly calm down, oil can fall fast and pull Exxon down with it. Watch the headlines.

### 3. Coinbase (COIN) — Crypto exchange — *Lean toward betting against it / avoid buying for now*
Coinbase is the biggest U.S. company for buying and selling cryptocurrency, so its stock tends to move with Bitcoin — only more violently. With Bitcoin dropping below $77,000 this morning and the growing fear that the Federal Reserve raises rates next week (which is bad for crypto), the wind is blowing against it today.
- **Why it matters today:** Higher interest rates pull money away from things like Bitcoin that pay no interest, and Coinbase feels that pain first.
- **In plain words:** This could be a good one to bet against if it keeps falling, and it's a risky one to buy right now. If next week's Fed meeting brings a rate hike, crypto-linked stocks could get hit harder. Only experienced traders comfortable with big swings should touch it either way.

### 4. RH (RH) — Luxury home furnishings — *Consider buying on the strong report*
RH (formerly Restoration Hardware) reported results after the market closed and blew past expectations — it earned $2.70 per share when analysts were looking for just $0.39, and sales came in ahead of forecasts thanks to strong demand for its new high-end "RH Estates" furniture collection and a good start for its new London store. At least one Wall Street firm raised its price target afterward.
- **Why it matters today:** A big, clear earnings beat like this often brings in buyers for several days as the good news sinks in.
- **The catch:** RH is a pricey, jumpy stock tied to how much wealthy people are spending on their homes, and management again flagged higher shipping and overseas-expansion costs. If the overall market turns sour on rate fears, even good news can get ignored.

### 5. GameStop (GME) — Video game retailer — *Speculative buy at most; treat as a gamble*
GameStop rose more than 3% before the open after its CEO, Ryan Cohen, disclosed he personally bought 1 million shares at about $20.375 each. When a company's boss puts his own money in, some investors take it as a vote of confidence and pile in.
- **Why it matters today:** A fresh, specific piece of news (the CEO's purchase) is exactly the kind of thing that draws in short-term buyers.
- **The catch:** GameStop is famous for wild, crowd-driven swings that have little to do with how the business is actually doing. These moves can reverse just as fast as they start. Keep any position tiny.

## Big Movers

*These are the stocks up or down roughly 40% or more today. Both are very small companies, and that makes them extremely dangerous — thinly-traded tiny stocks can crash just as fast as they spiked. Treat these as gambles, not investments, and keep them tiny if you touch them at all.*

- **Tenon Medical (TNON), up about 117% (around $5.30):** This tiny medical-device maker (it sells an implant that fuses a joint in the lower back) is soaring for a second day. It paid off about $5.16 million of debt in cash ahead of its due date, removing the risk that the debt turned into new shares that would water down existing owners. Its latest sales also jumped 127% from a year ago, though off a very small base. **Risk (very important): this is a micro-cap stock that has already more than doubled. Stocks that spike this hard on this little trading very often give a big part of it back within days. Do not chase it.**
- **Digital Brands Group (DBGI), up about 81% (around $6.79):** This small clothing-brand company is jumping after signaling a financial turnaround (it says it expects to start generating positive cash next month), on top of an earlier proposal from a shareholder to buy the whole company for $77.58 a share in cash. **Risk (very important): a takeover *proposal* is not a done deal, and a tiny stock like this can collapse fast if the deal falls through or the excitement fades. Extremely speculative — keep it tiny or skip it.**

*If any other tiny stock rockets 40% or more on a single headline later today, treat it with the same extreme caution. Thin trading means you can easily get stuck unable to sell at a good price.*
